## KQ1. Hyperscaler capex: 2025 actuals, 2026 guidance, 2027 signals, and debt funding

### Takeaway
Big-4 2026 capex guidance stepped up again at Q2-2026 earnings (July 2026) to roughly $710–730B combined versus ~$410B spent in 2025, with Amazon (~$220B) and Alphabet ($195–205B) raising and Alphabet explicitly guiding 2027 "significantly" higher; there is no sign of slowing in disclosed guidance, but free cash flow has turned negative at Alphabet and Amazon and the spend is increasingly funded by bonds, off-balance-sheet SPVs and not-yet-commenced leases.

### Cited Findings
**2025 base and 2026 aggregate**
- Combined big-four 2026 capex estimates range from ~$710B (April midpoints) to ~$730B (post-July guidance), versus roughly $410B spent in 2025 — [Analysis Atlas](https://analysis-atlas.com/research/hyperscaler-ai-capex-2026/); [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/big-tech-ai-capex-in-2025-microsoft-google-meta-amazon-and-the-spending-race); [Introl](https://introl.com/blog/hyperscaler-capex-690-billion-microsoft-azure-power-bottleneck-2026) (Introl's figure is $690B).
- Other aggregate figures circulating: ~$800B for 2026 hyperscaler capex (one bubble-skeptic source), $660–690B+ (another), and Goldman's "compute capex" estimate of $527B — definitions differ (compute-only vs. total capex vs. including leases) — [Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/when-will-the-ai-bubble-burst); [Luminix revenue hurdle](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/how-much-revenue-is-required-to-justify-the-ai-capex-buildout-and-avoid-a-bubble).
- Goldman Sachs is cited projecting hyperscaler capex nearing $1.14 trillion in 2027 (forecast, not guidance) — [IO Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-capex-1-trillion-estimates-too-low).

### Inferences
- Guidance trajectory through July 2026 is unambiguously upward (every big-4 raise in 2026 was a raise, none a cut), and Alphabet's "significantly higher 2027" plus TSMC's "2026–2028 capex > prior three years" imply the sell-side ~$1T+ 2027 figures are not out of line with company signaling.
- The qualitative shift in 2026 is that Alphabet and Amazon FCF went negative — the first time the capex cycle is no longer self-funded at the two most cash-generative names — which is why debt issuance, SPVs, and lease commitments (the ~$662B not-yet-commenced leases) have become the key fragility metric rather than capex itself.
- The precise "share funded by debt" cannot be stated from gathered sources; the directional evidence (Alphabet ~$100B debt raised, Oracle $40B raise vs. $70B capex, $120B+ SPV private credit, CoreWeave almost entirely debt-funded) suggests debt is now a material but still minority share for the big four and the majority for Oracle/neoclouds.

## KQ2. Component layers: pricing power vs. commoditization

### Takeaway
Pricing power in 2026 is concentrated in the upstream, capacity-constrained layers — HBM (contract prices up ~20% for 2026 with TrendForce forecasting a doubling in 2027), CoWoS (allocated, 52–78-week lead times), gas turbines/transformers (multi-year backlogs, +49% cost) and high-end optics (200G EML ~2x ASP of 100G) — while the integration layers (SMCI, Dell, HPE servers) show clear gross-margin compression as Nvidia captures most of the server bill of materials.

### Cited Findings
**GPUs / accelerators**
- Nvidia Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended July 26, 2026): revenue $96.2B (+106% YoY) vs. consensus $92.07B; Data Center $89.0B (+117% YoY, +18% QoQ, ~92% of revenue); gross margin 75% GAAP and non-GAAP; GAAP EPS $2.46; Q3 FY27 guide ~$108B ±2% with no China data-center compute assumed; Vera Rubin ramping into full production at CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Azure, OCI, Nebius — [StorageNewsletter](https://www.storagenewsletter.com/2026/08/27/nvidia-fiscal-2q27-financial-results/); [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/nvidia-operating-income-jumps-124-to-63-7-billion-as-revenue-doubles-and-q3-outlook-hits-108-billion/); [RuntimeWire](https://runtimewire.com/article/nvidia-hits-96-2-billion-revenue-as-data-center-demand-surges-117).
- Sub-segment split (single source, Webull citing Nvidia release): Hyperscale $48.7B (+102%), AI Clouds/Industrial/Enterprise $40.3B (+138%), Edge $7.2B (+27%) — [Webull](https://www.webull.com/blog/304-Nvidia-Q2-FY2027-Earnings-Beats-Revenue-EPS-Estimates-Guides-Q3-to-108B).
- Nvidia operating income $63.7B, +124% YoY — [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/nvidia-operating-income-jumps-124-to-63-7-billion-as-revenue-doubles-and-q3-outlook-hits-108-billion/).
- Nvidia's 75% gross margin and 2x revenue growth in the same quarter that SMCI prints 9% gross margin is the clearest value-capture datapoint in the stack (see servers below).

**Memory / HBM**
- TrendForce (Dec 2025): Samsung and SK hynix raised HBM3E supply prices by nearly 20% for 2026 — "unusual" for a category set by fixed annual agreements — [IntuitionLabs summarizing TrendForce](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/hbm-dram-ai-memory-demand).
- Silicon Analysts (June 27, 2026): HBM3E contract prices rose 20% as demand outstrips supply — [Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/market/hbm3e-contract-prices-rise-20-as-ai-memory-demand-outstrips-supply-2026-06-27).
- TrendForce forecast: HBM contract prices could double in 2027 as the DRAM shortage intensifies; memory makers hold "absolute pricing power" (forecast) — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/yZ5NjJ4BtCxy99G5a4Ti); [BigGo Finance 2](https://finance.biggo.com/news/R2qLh54BYH_ypPqOvy4R).
- HBM share of total DRAM wafer input: 18% (2025) → 22% (2026) → ~30% (2027) per TrendForce — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/hbm-dram-ai-memory-demand).
- HBM4 12-high 36GB stack pricing: ~$600/stack in 2026, roughly double next year per Seoul Economic Daily industry estimates; Silicon Analysts' own reference ~$550/stack — [Silicon Analysts HBM pricing](https://siliconanalysts.com/data/hbm-pricing).
- Amazon attributed its $20B capex raise mainly to higher memory-chip costs (demand-side confirmation of memory pricing power) — [Vectorshift](https://vectorshift.ai/research/companies/amazon-com/earnings/2026-q2).
- SK hynix Q3-2026 due Oct 29, 2026; FnGuide consensus operating profit ~76.96T won (vs. 11.38T won in Q3-2025, the first >10T won quarter); Citi trimmed to 74T won on won strength; HBM4 for Vera Rubin fully reflected from Q3; risk flagged that Samsung's HBM4 ramp dilutes SK hynix's supply premium — [Money Today](https://www.mt.co.kr/en/industry/2026/10/19/2026101913371799572); [S&P Global (Aug 5, 2026 Q2 note)](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/research/2026/08/sk-hynix-postq-profit-beat-offsets-revenue-miss).
- HBM backlogs push data-center GPU lead times to 36–52 weeks (secondary source) — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/is-the-ai-chip-shortage-over-in-2026-gpu-pricing-and-what-comes-next).

**Foundry / packaging**
- TSMC Q3-2026 (reported Oct 15, 2026): revenue NT$1.49T (+50.9% YoY in NT$; ~$46.7–46.9B USD, ~+41%); net income NT$763.06B (+68.7%), sixth straight record; Q4 guide $52.3–54.3B; HPC 68% of revenue (+22% QoQ); 2026 capex held at $60–64B (raised in July from a range topping at $52–56B); management says 2026–2028 capex will exceed the prior three years and keep rising with demand; CoWoS demand "remained strong," TSMC will "work with customers to keep CoWoS capacity sufficient" — [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202610150018); [CRN Asia](https://www.crnasia.com/news/2026/components-and-peripherals/tsmc-third-quarter-revenue-tops-guidance-as-ceo-says-ai-dema); [XTB](https://www.xtb.com/sca-en/market-analysis/news-and-research/tsmc-breaks-records-once-again-driven-by-the-ai-boom).

Not reliable; TSMC gives no official figure.

**Optics / networking**
- Lumentum FQ4-2026: revenue $1.006B, +109.3% YoY; FQ1-27 guide $1.225–1.275B; 200G EMLs for 1.6T carry ~2x the ASP of 100G EMLs used in 800G; component tightness keeps shipments below demand — [Semiconductor Today](https://semiconductor-today.com/news_items/2026/aug/lumentum-190826.shtml); [MEXC/Lumentum](https://www.mexc.com/crypto-pulse/article/lumentum-earnings-confirm-ai-optical-demand-138372).

### Inferences
- Pricing power ranking (strongest to weakest) supported by evidence: Nvidia (75% GM, +117% DC growth) ≈ HBM makers (+20% contract price, doubling forecast) > TSMC/CoWoS (allocated, record margins, no price data) > turbines/transformers (multi-year backlogs, +49% cost) > high-end optics (1.6T ASP uplift but component-constrained) > servers/ODMs (single-digit to low-teens GM, falling).
- Memory is the layer whose pricing power is *newly* visible in 2026 — Amazon's capex raise being memory-driven is a demand-side confirmation — but it is also the most cyclical; TrendForce's own framing ("could double") is a forecast and the Samsung HBM4 ramp is the stated risk.
- The server/ODM layer is behaving like a classic pass-through: revenue growth is enormous (Dell $30B AI orders YTD) but economics accrue to the component supplier.

## KQ3. Power: shortfall size, equipment lead times/pricing, and whether power deals kept scaling in 2026

### Takeaway
Power equipment is the most stretched physical bottleneck in 2026: GE Vernova's gas backlog grew from 83 GW (end-2025) to ~116 GW (Q2-2026) with deliveries being booked for 2031, transformer lead times run 128–208 weeks, and trackers estimate only ~5 GW of data-center capacity under active construction against 12–16 GW committed for 2026; nuclear PPAs continued (Vistra–Meta 2.6 GW, Talen–Amazon 1.92 GW/$18B) but behind-the-meter gas has hit local-permitting and FERC friction.

### Cited Findings
**Demand / shortfall**
- Goldman Sachs projects US data-center power demand of 31 GW (2025) → 41 GW (2026) → 66 GW (2027) — [Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/data-centre-power-demand-pipeline-october-2026/).
- One tracker estimates ~5 GW under active construction vs. 12–16 GW operators committed to deliver in 2026, framed as a "7 GW capacity crisis" with delays and cancellations (lower-tier source) — [Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/us-ai-data-center-delays-cancellations-7gw-capacity-crisis-2026/).
- Introl frames power as the binding constraint on Microsoft Azure capacity in 2026 — [Introl](https://introl.com/blog/hyperscaler-capex-690-billion-microsoft-azure-power-bottleneck-2026).

**Gas turbines**
- GE Vernova ended Q2-2026 with ~116 GW of gas backlog plus slot reservations (up from 100 GW three months earlier and 83 GW at end-2025); CEO says it is taking orders for 2031 delivery; expects ≥125 GW under contract by December 2026 — [Yahoo Finance / Oilprice](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/gas-turbine-shortage-just-became-210000180.html); [Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/The-Gas-Turbine-Shortage-Just-Became-AIs-Biggest-Constraint.html).
- Siemens Energy gas backlog 69 GW; lead times three years or more; combined-cycle plant lead times ~five years; BloombergNEF (cited) says lead times jumped from ~3.5 years in 2023 and costs rose 49% — [Oilprice global shortage](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/US-Power-Boom-Triggers-Global-Gas-Turbine-Shortage.html).

**Transformers / grid equipment**
- PwC (via pv magazine, May 11, 2026): lead times for high-capacity transformers as long as four years — [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/).
- Generator step-up transformer lead times >160 weeks in Q1-2026; high-voltage circuit-breaker lead times 125 weeks vs. 77 weeks in 2023 — [HuggingNews](https://huggingnews.com/ai/transformer-lead-times-top-160-weeks-for-us-ai-data-centers-b0250047).
- Standard power transformers average ~128 weeks to delivery (different source/definition) — [GO-IBR](https://go-ibr.com/energy/new-datacenters-cant-launch-because-of-transformers-shortage/).
- Wood Mackenzie: GSU transformer demand +274% between 2019 and 2025 — [HuggingNews](https://huggingnews.com/ai/transformer-lead-times-top-160-weeks-for-us-ai-data-centers-b0250047).
- JLL: data-center equipment lead times reached 33 weeks in 2026, ~50% longer than pre-2020; electrical equipment described as the development constraint — [Build.inc](https://build.inc/insights/data-center-transformer-procurement-2026).

**Power deals (did they keep scaling in 2026?)**
- Vistra: three 20-year PPAs with Meta totaling 2.6 GW incl. uprates (Perry, Davis-Besse, Beaver Valley; 433 MW uprates; announced Jan 9, 2026); Vistra also buying 5.5 GW of gas assets via a $4.7B Cogentrix deal to position for the 2026 "reliability gap" — [Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/blog/vistra-doubles-down-on-data-centers); [Gravel2Gavel tracker (May 15, 2026)](https://www.gravel2gavel.com/files/2026/05/Data-Center-Project-Tracker-05-15-26.pdf).
