## Key Question 1: Ad platforms — how much of Meta/Google/Amazon/AppLovin growth is AI-attributable, and are agencies losing?

### Takeaway
The large ad platforms are the clearest AI "winners" in this universe: Meta is growing ads ~26-28% with a company-stated $75B Advantage+ run-rate and double-digit conversion lifts, Google Search is growing 17-19%, Amazon Ads +26%, and AppLovin +53%. The losers are the neutral intermediaries and holding companies: The Trade Desk is down ~68% YTD 2026 with its first-ever guided revenue decline, and WPP is shrinking (H1 2026 LFL -4.7%) while its CEO explicitly warns AI is "deflationary" for agency pricing; Omnicom and Publicis are growing only via scale/consolidation.

### Inferences
- The AI-attributable share of platform growth cannot be isolated from disclosures; Meta's only hard anchor is the Advantage+ run-rate ($60B at Q3 2025 to $75B at Q2 2026 = ~+25% in three quarters, roughly tracking total ad growth), which suggests AI automation is the delivery mechanism rather than an incremental growth layer. Price-per-ad +12% with impressions +14% is the strongest evidence that AI targeting is raising auction yield, not just volume.
- Structural reason for platform gains: proprietary first-party conversion data plus closed-loop measurement; AI models compound on that data. AppLovin's Q2 miss shows the flip side — growth is now hostage to the cadence of model improvements.
- Structural reason for TTD/agency losses: no proprietary data moat; walled gardens are internalizing creative and media-buying (Advantage+, Performance Max, Amazon DSP pricing), and AI creative tools let clients demand that productivity gains be rebated (WPP's own "deflationary" language). Omnicom's growth is a consolidation/synergy story, not an AI uplift story.

### Inferences
- The "data moat vs LLM" debate is so far being decided by the market, not the P&L: the incumbents' growth held or accelerated (TRI Legal at 10% organic is above its historical mid-single digits) while multiples compressed. The structural edge for Westlaw/Lexis is proprietary, citable primary-law content, editorial enhancements (KeyCite/Shepard's), regulatory need for authoritative citation, and workflow lock-in; the market's fear is that frontier-model vendors with vertical plugins collapse the application layer.
- Harvey's growth (~2x ARR in three quarters) and law-firm revenue growth coexist, which implies AI spend is currently additive for firms (new cost line, no fee rebate) rather than cannibalistic; the deflation risk sits with clients forcing alternative fee arrangements later.
- LegalZoom is the clearest case of AI search disintermediation in legal: its funnel is Google-dependent consumer discovery, not proprietary data.
- Tax prep shows the "assisted" tier gaining (Intuit TurboTax Live, HRB assisted pricing up) — regulation (IRS filing, liability) and consumer trust keep pricing power for the human-in-the-loop tier.

## Key Question 9: Pricing power vs deflation, and what is priced in (valuation, YTD 2026 moves)

### Takeaway
Pricing power (price per ad +12% at Meta; HRB assisted pricing up; TRI/RELX legal ~10% organic; MSCI index fees +18%) sits with owners of proprietary transaction/intent data, benchmarks and regulated credentials; deflation sits with sellers of substitutable text/labor (Fiverr, Upwork, Chegg, agencies, LegalZoom's search funnel, Gartner research). The market has priced disruption aggressively and indiscriminately in 2026 (TTD -68% YTD, Accenture -50% over 12 months, RELX worst day since 1988, S&P Global at ~19.9x forward below peers), creating a gap between reported fundamentals and multiples for the data incumbents.
