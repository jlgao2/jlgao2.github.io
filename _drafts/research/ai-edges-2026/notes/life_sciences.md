**Headline verdict on the user's prior ("contract research labs bullish, general pharma mixed"):**
- *CROs bullish* is supported by the **bookings/backlog data** (IQVIA Q2 book-to-bill 1.22x, Medpace record Q3 awards, Charles River and Fortrea raising guidance), but the **AI-specific causal link is thin**: no CRO has disclosed an audited, group-level cycle-time, cost or margin number attributable to AI. The measurable AI items are workflow-level (ICON SmartDraft "30% contract-negotiation-time reduction"; IQVIA "294 agents / 90 use cases"). CRO strength in 2026 is better explained by the biotech funding recovery (EBP funding ~$35B in Q2 2026, >2x YoY per IQVIA citing BioWorld) than by AI.
- *General pharma mixed* is strongly supported: the quantified AI numbers in pharma are **cost-side** (Pfizer ~$500M R&D savings in 2025 per its 2026 proxy; "16,000 scientist hours/year"; Sanofi "50% early R&D cost reduction" [company claim]) and are small relative to the macro headwinds (Section 232 100% tariff regime with company-deal carve-outs, MFN deals covering 89% of branded market, patent cliff). AI has **not** changed the pharma revenue line.
- *AI drug discovery* now has a hard datapoint: **the first generative-AI-discovered drug entered Phase 3 (Insilico rentosertib, GENESIS-IPF-3, Sept 2026)**, but no AI-discovered drug is approved, Phase 2 success rates (~40%) look like historical norms, and Isomorphic has slipped to "end-2026" for a first IND. Classify as (b) credible near-term for a few named assets, (c) hype for platform-level claims of changed probability-of-success.

## Key Question 3: Where is AI changing the economics of clinical trials (synthetic control arms, FDA guidance 2025-2026, FDA's own AI use e.g. Elsa)?

### Takeaway
Regulatory AI is moving but not yet changing trial economics: FDA's January 2025 draft "credibility framework" for AI in regulatory decision-making remained draft as of September 2026; FDA/EMA issued joint non-binding principles in January 2026; ICH M15 (model-informed drug development) was finalised by FDA in June 2026. Fully synthetic control arms have not been accepted as the basis for approval; externally controlled trials remain rare (two in FY2023, zero in FY2024, four in FY2025). FDA's own Elsa tool reached version 4.0 with HALO data integration in May 2026, but FDA does not disclose when it uses AI on a submission.

### Cited Findings
- FDA's Jan 2025 draft guidance ("Considerations for the Use of AI to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products") still labelled "Draft Level 1 Guidance. Not for implementation" on 3 Sept 2026; many sponsors expected a final in H1 2026 and FDA gave no date. The framework is a 7-step, risk-based, context-of-use credibility assessment; it explicitly excludes early discovery and internal operational-efficiency uses — [Sakara Digital](https://sakaradigital.com/blog/fda-ai-credibility-guidance-still-draft-what-to-do/); [IntuitionLabs 7-step](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/fda-ai-drug-development-guidance); [Katalyze AI](https://www.katalyzeai.com/blog/what-fda-s-draft-ai-guidance-asks-for-and-what-it-leaves-out)
- Jan 2026: FDA and EMA jointly published 10 guiding principles for AI in drug development (human-centric, risk-based, data governance, transparency) — [Skadden FDLI report, May 2026](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/report-from-fdli-annual-meeting); [Drug Discovery News](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/regulating-ai-in-drug-discovery-what-fda-ema-and-ich-guidance-means-for-pharma-r-d-17366)
- ICH M15 (MIDD) reached Step 4 on 29 Jan 2026 and was issued by FDA as final guidance in June 2026 — [Drug Discovery News](https://www.drugdiscoverynews.com/regulating-ai-in-drug-discovery-what-fda-ema-and-ich-guidance-means-for-pharma-r-d-17366)
- FDA Elsa: launched June 2025 [older]; Elsa 4.0 and HALO integration announced 6 May 2026; HALO consolidates >40 application/submission data sources; Elsa 4.0 runs in FedRAMP High and "does not train on data submitted by regulated industry"; FDA has **not** proposed disclosing to applicants when Elsa is used on their submissions — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/report-from-fdli-annual-meeting); [IntuitionLabs HALO/Elsa guide](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/fda-halo-elsa-4-migration-guide); [JSCDM opinion paper](https://www.jscdm.org/article/id/503/)
- Unverified: a Substack post claims a Feb 2026 directive moved Elsa's model from Anthropic to Google — not corroborated — [Silverman Substack](https://steveilverman.substack.com/p/fda-is-using-ai-to-review-your-devices)
- External/synthetic controls: FDA's Feb 2023 draft on externally controlled trials remains the operative (draft) guidance; EMA has only a concept paper; FDA study-design tally shows externally controlled trials in NDA/BLA data of 2 (FY2023), 0 (FY2024), 4 (FY2025) — [IntuitionLabs virtual control arms](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/virtual-control-arms-clinical-trials); [PharmaSight on EMA qualification](https://www.pharmasight.tech/blog/digital-twins-external-control-arms-procova/)
- Unlearn's PROCOVA is EMA-qualified for Phase 2/3 with continuous outcomes, but it is a covariate-adjustment (variance-reduction) method, not a replacement control arm; accounts differ on FDA's stance (one says FDA declined a PROCOVA Letter of Intent into ISTAND because existing covariate guidance already covers it; Unlearn describes "positive feedback") — [Unlearn Substack](https://unlearnai.substack.com/p/built-on-regulatory-rigor-advancing); [PharmaSight](https://www.pharmasight.tech/blog/digital-twins-external-control-arms-procova/); May 2026 Unlearn methods preprint on digital twins as synthetic controls in single-arm trials — [arXiv 2605.12832](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.12832)
- "True synthetic control arms (generated by models) have not yet been accepted in regulatory submissions" — [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/interviews/synthetic-control-arms-full-impact-yet-to-be-realised/) (interview; secondary)
- Flagged as unreliable: aidrugsearch.com / theneuralbase.com claims (e.g., "15% of 2025 NDAs used synthetic arms", "mandatory third-party audits for high-risk models") not found in FDA primary text.

### Inferences
- The regulatory posture is "permissive but unfinalised": sponsors can use AI in submissions under the draft credibility framework, but the absence of a final guidance keeps most uses as supportive evidence rather than pivotal. This caps the near-term economic impact on trial cost/duration (control-arm replacement is the big lever and is not available).
- Elsa/HALO are more likely to change FDA *review throughput* and inspection targeting than sponsor economics in 2026-2027; the non-disclosure stance means sponsors cannot design submissions around it.
- Where AI *is* plausibly affecting trial economics today is operational (site selection, protocol optimisation, data cleaning, medical writing) inside CROs and sponsors — but, per Q1, nobody has published the numbers.

### Gaps
- No 2026 FDA statistic on how many submissions included AI components (FDA had reported ~500 cumulative AI-containing submissions through 2024 in older materials; no 2026 update found).
- Medidata's synthetic-control product uptake: no 2026 data found.
