# AI Value Flows to Volume, Not Seats

As of 26 October 2026, AI is adding measurable value in a narrow set of places that share one structure: they sell scarce physical capacity or charge by consumption rather than by seat, hour or click, and they own data the models cannot substitute. The hard numbers sit upstream (Nvidia's **$89.0B data-center quarter at 75% gross margin**, HBM contract prices **up ~20%**, GE Vernova's **116 GW gas-turbine backlog**), in consumption-priced data platforms (Snowflake re-accelerating to **+37%**, Palantir US commercial **+149%**), in first-party-data ad platforms (Meta **price per ad +12%**) and in a few regulated buyers of AI (JPMorgan's **~$2B of savings matching ~$2B of spend**, Lemonade's **5% loss-adjustment-expense ratio**). Value is being destroyed, in reported revenue rather than just multiples, where the product is substitutable text or labour sold by the unit: IT outsourcing (TCS repricing **~50% of engagements down 10-20%**), call-centre BPO (Concentrix **$1.05B goodwill impairment**), consumer Q&A (Chegg **-51%**), gig platforms (Fiverr writing/translation **>-24%**) and ad intermediaries (The Trade Desk **-68% YTD**). The stated prior is half right: CROs are genuinely bullish on fundamentals (IQVIA book-to-bill **1.22x**, Medpace **record awards**), but no CRO has disclosed a group-level AI effect and the upcycle is explained by biotech funding (**~$35B in Q2 2026, more than double a year earlier**), so "CROs bullish because of AI" is narrative, while "general pharma mixed" is strongly supported because Pfizer's **~$500M** of AI-linked savings is a rounding error against tariffs, most-favoured-nation pricing and the patent cliff. The market has already sorted stocks along almost exactly this line, so the obvious trade is largely priced (consumption software at 20-80x sales, Korean memory still **+97% YTD** after a 31% October drop, CrowdStrike at **~208x forward earnings**); the places where fundamentals and multiples diverge most are the de-rated seat-SaaS names at 10-15x forward earnings and the legal and financial data incumbents still growing ~10% organically. The dominant risk has moved from demand to financing: 2026 hyperscaler capex of **~$710-730B**, free cash flow negative at Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, hyperscalers at **~10% of global investment-grade issuance**, CoreWeave bonds yielding **~15-16%**, Oracle cut to **BBB-** and **-63% from its high**, and the IMF naming an AI correction one of three salient stability risks. A portfolio rebuilt around these edges should tilt to volume-priced scarce complements at ordinary multiples and to moated buyers of AI, hold the consumption-priced winners in small size, treat the de-rated incumbents as a contrarian sleeve, avoid or short junk-financed compute and unit-priced labour sellers, and hedge the implicit AI-capex bet inside any cap-weighted index. This report is research for a thoughtful individual investor, not personalised financial advice; it flags throughout where the evidence is thin, and the underlying notes were built from search summaries rather than full primary filings, with several key Q3 prints (IQVIA, ICON, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon) still due in the week of 27 October.

## Productivity is up and sector-concentrated, but attribution to AI remains unproven

The first thing to separate is what is measured from what is claimed. At the economy level the measured fact is that US productivity has accelerated: BLS puts the current cycle's annualised growth at **2.1% versus 1.5% for 2007-2019**, with Q2 2026 running **2.2% year on year**, and BLS itself says it captures AI only implicitly through software capital ([BLS Productivity and Costs Q2 2026](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/pdf/prod2.pdf)). The Dallas Fed finds labour productivity growing at a **2.4% annualised rate since early 2024** and judges the international pattern "appears related to high AI use" ([Dallas Fed, July 2026](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/economics/2026/0707)); a secondary breakdown of the same work has finance, professional services and information, only 16% of hours, delivering **3.7% annualised productivity growth** ([Dataporium](https://dataporium.tech/articles/productivity-growth-and-ai-in-2026-what-the-bls-data-shows-so-far)). That concentration in the most AI-exposed sectors is consistent with an AI contribution but does not prove it, and the Atlanta Fed's executive survey adds the telling detail that firm-reported productivity gains (mean **3.0%** for 2026) run well ahead of the implied measure (**1.8%**), with gains "not primarily driven by firms' capital deepening" ([Atlanta Fed WP 2026-04](https://www.atlantafed.org/-/media/Project/Atlanta/FRBA/Documents/research/publication/working-paper/2026/03/25/04-artificial-intelligence-productivity-and-the-workforce-evidence-from-corporate-executives.pdf)). The gap between what executives say and what shows up in output is the first quantitative hint of AI-washing.

At the firm level the picture is bimodal. Adoption is real but shallower than the headlines: Census BTOS has roughly **20% of US businesses** using AI in production as of May 2026, rising to ~37% for firms with 250+ employees ([St. Louis Fed FRED blog, Oct 2026](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/blog/2026/10/which-businesses-are-using-ai-technology)), while Ramp's card data shows **55.9%** of its customers paying for AI tools in September 2026, with Ramp itself titling its last two monthly editions "Cracks in the AI thesis" because new adoption is decelerating ([Ramp AI Index, Sept 2026](https://ramp.com/data/ai-index-sept-2026)). Self-reported returns are weak: McKinsey's 2026 survey, per secondary summaries, has **37%** of respondents attributing any EBIT impact to AI and only **~6%** as high performers, meaning roughly 63% report no measurable earnings effect ([summary](https://www.arturmarkus.com/enterprise-ai-roi-5-numbers-everyone-quotes-wrong-from-95-fail-to-mckinseys-37/)). Goldman's Jim Covello, the original sceptic, conceded in June 2026 that consumer adoption "far exceeded" his expectations while insisting "at some point you've got to make money" ([Fortune, 5 June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/05/is-ai-a-bubble-worth-it-short-term-early-goldman-skeptic-covello-profits/)). The honest reading is that value is measurable where tasks are high-volume, text- or code-heavy and instrumented (coding, customer support, document processing, back-office finance) and unmeasured almost everywhere else.

Who has captured the money so far is not ambiguous. Nvidia reported **$96.2B of revenue (+106%)** and **$89.0B of data-center revenue (+117%)** at a **75% gross margin** for the quarter ended July 2026, guiding to ~$108B next quarter with no China assumed ([Nvidia Q2 FY27](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/default.aspx)). Annualised, Nvidia's data-center line alone (~$356B) is roughly three times the combined run-rate of the two largest model labs: Anthropic reported **~$65B annualised** at end-July 2026 ([CNBC, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-says-annualized-revenue-climbed-to-65-billion-in-july.html)) and OpenAI roughly **$40-50B**, while burning cash against **~$665B of compute commitments** and, per an FT-reported internal plan, **negative free cash flow of $278B over 2026-2030** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/openai/); [reporting summary](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/openai-revenue-losses-and-cash-burn-what-has-been-reported)). The application layer has a handful of breakouts (Cursor ~**$4B** annualised, acquired by SpaceX for ~$60B) but is one to two orders of magnitude smaller than infrastructure spend ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/134107-cursor-tops-4b-annualized-revenue/)). Sequoia's David Cahn has escalated his "required revenue" series from $200B in 2023 to **$1.5T in July 2026**, with secondary coverage putting the revenue needed to justify 2026 spend near **$3T** ([Cahn](https://dcahn.substack.com/p/ais-15t-question); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/sequoias-cahn-pegs-ais-2026-revenue-gap-at-roughly-3t)). The gap is widening despite lab revenue growing 3-5x a year, because capex compounds faster. That is the macro frame for every edge below: AI is an input whose price is collapsing (per-token prices at fixed capability fall roughly **10x a year**; the Silicon Data index fell from **$2.04 to ~$1.17 per million tokens between 31 May and 8 August 2026**), so durable value accrues to whoever owns a scarce complement, a demand aggregator, or a non-AI moat, and flows away from anyone selling the deflating input in the middle ([arXiv 2601.12339](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12339); [SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3363549/enterprise-ai-costs-hit-2026-low-driven-price-wars-chinese-open-source-models-research)).

## Testing the prior: CROs are bullish on biotech funding, not on AI; "pharma mixed" holds

The stated prior, "contract research organisations bullish, general pharma mixed," survives on fundamentals but fails on attribution. The measured outcome for CROs is strong: IQVIA's R&D Solutions booked **$3.15B of net new business in Q2 2026 (+19%), a 1.22x book-to-bill**, with contracted backlog of **$34.2B** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/iqvia-holdings-inc-iqv-q2-210045582.html)); Medpace printed **record net new awards of $873.4M (+10.6%)** in Q3 and raised full-year EPS guidance to $17.97-18.32 ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/medpace-q3-2026-slides-record-awards-fuel-profit-beat-guidance-raised-93CH-4966599)); Charles River and Fortrea both raised guidance ([CRL Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001100682/000110068226000115/crl2q26earningsrelease.htm); [Fortrea](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/fortrea-q2-earnings-call-highlights-150454455.html)). But the causal story the companies themselves give is the funding cycle: IQVIA cited emerging-biopharma funding of **~$35B in Q2 2026, more than double the prior year**, which "typically converts into CRO awards six months to three quarters later" ([NextMSC summary of IQVIA call](https://www.nextmsc.com/news/recent-mergers-launches-and-partnerships-in-the-cro-services-industry)). The AI content of CRO results is company claim at best: IQVIA talks about **294 AI agents across 90 use cases**, ICON's only quantified metric is a **30% reduction in contract-negotiation time** from its SmartDraft tool, and no CRO has disclosed an audited cycle-time, cost-per-patient or margin effect attributable to AI ([Yahoo IQVIA preview](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/iqvia-gears-post-q3-earnings-125800801.html); [FactorsToday on ICON](https://www.factorstoday.com/research/ICLR)). The sharpest piece of evidence is an absence: Medpace, the margin leader, posted record awards without any AI commentary at all, which shows AI is not a necessary input to CRO outperformance in 2026. The CRO partnerships that exist (ICON with Anthropic and Microsoft, IQVIA with NVIDIA, Charles River joining Lilly's TuneLab) position CROs as distribution channels for other people's models rather than owners of proprietary ones, so the plausible long-run AI effect is labour-cost takeout showing up in margin, which has not happened yet, against a plausible risk that cheaper trial operations push sponsors toward fixed-price contracts ([ICON press releases](https://www.iconplc.com/news-events/press-releases); [CRL/TuneLab](https://ir.criver.com/news-releases/news-release-details/charles-river-joins-lilly-tunelab-optimize-aiml-platform-through)). A governance caveat sits on ICON specifically: it restated results and disclosed control weaknesses in its 20-F ([StockTitan 20-F summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/ICLR/20-f-icon-plc-files-annual-report-foreign-issuer-b5973eee8846.html)). Verdict: CROs are a good funding-cycle long with AI as free optionality, and the IQVIA and ICON prints on 27-28 October are the first chance for an AI-attributed metric that is not already in the price, because what is priced is the cycle, not AI.

"General pharma mixed" is better supported than the user may expect. The only board-level, filing-grade AI number is Pfizer's 2026 proxy crediting **~$500M of 2025 R&D savings** to its AI/digital programme, with the CEO calling AI "the main lever" of cost cuts ([Pfizer 2026 proxy](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/78003/000007800326000033/pfe-proxy2026courtesy.pdf); [BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/pfizer-cost-cuts-savings-2q-2026-earnings/826930/)). Everything else is company claim: Sanofi's "50% lower early-stage R&D cost," AstraZeneca's ">50% faster" early target design ([IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/pharma-digital-transformation-leaders)). Lilly and Novo are spending, not reporting returns: Lilly's **up to $1B** NVIDIA co-innovation lab and **1,016-GPU "LillyPod"** disclosed no productivity metric, and Lilly's own TuneLab executive conceded a model "may be obsolete by the time an asset reaches the clinic" ([NVIDIA release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-and-Lilly-Announce-Co-Innovation-AI-Lab-to-Reinvent-Drug-Discovery-in-the-Age-of-AI/default.aspx); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/business/ai-is-changing-pharmas-bottom-line-now-but-not-through-splashy-drug-discovery)). Against a few hundred million of cost-side savings per company, the sector faces a Section 232 regime with a **100% default tariff on patented drugs** (0% for the 26 companies with MFN agreements covering **89% of the branded market**), IRA pricing and the patent cliff ([Clinical Leader](https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/u-s-pharma-tariffs-and-mfn-become-law-after-april-update-0001); [Pharmaphorum](https://pharmaphorum.com/market-access/one-year-after-mfn-how-us-policy-reshaping-global-pharma-model)). AI modestly improves pharma's cost denominator and has not touched its revenue line; the stock dispersion (Lilly roughly **+19% YTD**, Novo **down more than 25%**) is about GLP-1s, not AI ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/19/prediction-eli-lilly-stock-could-be-a-long-term-winner-for-investors/); [Yahoo on Novo](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/novo-nordisk-growth-story-under-154102050.html)).

Where AI in life sciences is actually monetising is one layer down, at the software and platform level, and this is the part of the prior worth upgrading. Veeva grew revenue **18% to $928M** with its CEO calling it Vault CRM's "best quarter ever" alongside AI-agent launches, and the stock is **+44.5% YTD** ([Veeva 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001393052/000139305226000032/veev-20260731xex991.htm); [Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/stock/veev/articles/620171/guidance-up-trend-intact-veeva-is-todays-double-confirmation-stock/2026-10-23)); Schrödinger's software ACV rose **27%** and the stock is **+53% YTD**; Insilico posted its **first profitable half-year (~$106M revenue)** on licensing and software ([OnHealthcare.tech](https://www.onhealthcare.tech/p/the-ai-drug-discovery-capital-stack); [Fool SDGR](https://www.fool.com/quote/nasdaq/sdgr/); [Patsnap summary](https://synapse.patsnap.com/drug/ff292d7fe5734659ad53a3e0d8756c2b)). The AI-discovery pipeline itself has one hard milestone, Insilico's rentosertib becoming the first generative-AI-designed drug to enter Phase 3 in September 2026, but its Phase 2a saw seven liver-related discontinuations, no readout is likely before 2028, the best success-rate data (Phase 2 ~40%) matches historical norms, and Isomorphic has slipped its first trial to "end of 2026" ([Insilico](https://insilico.com/news/isn1009261-insilico-medicine-doses-first-patient-genesis-ipf-3); [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03743-2); [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ai-discovered-drugs-clinical-trials-2026); [Reuters via Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/googlebacked-ai-drug-discovery-startup-isomorphic-labs-delays-clinical-trial-timeline-4456134)). Pure-pipeline AI biotechs are burning cash into unchanged clinical odds (Absci's quarterly revenue fell to **$0.3M**; Recursion cut four of eleven programmes), and ProMarket's "wrong bottleneck" critique, that discovery is ~10-15% of drug cost while the clinic is the constraint, stands ([PDP Spectra](https://pdpspectra.com/blog/ai-drug-discovery-platforms-2026/); [ProMarket](https://www.promarket.org/2026/07/02/pharmas-ai-boom-has-bet-on-the-wrong-bottleneck/)). Regulation caps near-term trial economics: FDA's January 2025 AI credibility framework was still draft in September 2026, and fully synthetic control arms have not been accepted in any pivotal submission ([Sakara Digital](https://sakaradigital.com/blog/fda-ai-credibility-guidance-still-draft-what-to-do/); [Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/interviews/synthetic-control-arms-full-impact-yet-to-be-realised/)). Tools companies are the same story as CROs: Thermo Fisher beat and raised on a biotech recovery but explicitly deferred quantifying AI's contribution to a future investor day ([Fool TMO transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/10/21/thermo-fisher-tmo-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Revised prior: CROs and tools are a cyclical long with no AI evidence; pharma is mixed with AI as a small cost tailwind; life-sciences software is the measured AI winner in the group; AI-discovery biotechs are narrative.

The top of the table is where measurement is least ambiguous because the sellers of scarce physical inputs report price and margin, not "AI ARR." Nvidia's 75% gross margin in the same quarter Super Micro printed **9.3%** gross margin, and Dell gave up **140bp** of gross margin on "a more competitive pricing environment" while booking **$30B of AI server orders**, is the cleanest value-capture datapoint in the whole stack: the integration layer is a pass-through, the component layer is the toll booth ([Fintool/Goldman on SMCI](https://fintool.com/news/goldman-smci-sell-margin-erosion); [Nasdaq/Zacks on Dell](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/dells-margins-under-pressure-can-isg-strength-drive-rebound)). Memory's pricing power is newly visible in 2026 and demand-side confirmed by Amazon attributing its **$20B capex raise** mainly to higher memory costs, with TrendForce forecasting HBM contract prices could double in 2027, but it is also the most cyclical layer and the Samsung HBM4 ramp is the stated risk to SK hynix's premium ([Vectorshift on Amazon Q2](https://vectorshift.ai/research/companies/amazon-com/earnings/2026-q2); [Silicon Analysts](https://siliconanalysts.com/market/hbm3e-contract-prices-rise-20-as-ai-memory-demand-outstrips-supply-2026-06-27); [Money Today on SK hynix](https://www.mt.co.kr/en/industry/2026/10/19/2026101913371799572)). TSMC's Q3 was its sixth straight record with HPC at 68% of revenue and management saying 2026-2028 capex will exceed the prior three years ([Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/business/202610150018)); the Taiwan supply chain's monthly revenue disclosures are the most verifiable follow-through anywhere outside the US, with Hon Hai's September revenue **+38%** (its first NT$1T month), Quanta **+77%** and Wiwynn roughly **doubling** ([Hon Hai monthly report](https://image.honhai.com/upload/202610/financy_by_month/Hon_Hai_Monthly_Revenue_Report_September_2026_EN_20261005_2422.pdf); [Quanta monthly sales](https://www.quantatw.com/quanta/english/investment/financials_ms.aspx); [Wiwynn](https://www.wiwynn.com/investors)).

Power is the most stretched physical bottleneck and the most interesting second-order edge because its demand depends on data-center capex volume rather than on AI pricing. GE Vernova's gas backlog plus slot reservations went from 83 GW at end-2025 to **~116 GW** by Q2 2026, with the CEO taking orders for 2031 delivery, Q2 orders of **$24.2B (+88% organic)** and data-center orders **more than twice all of 2025** ([Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/The-Gas-Turbine-Shortage-Just-Became-AIs-Biggest-Constraint.html); [GreentechLead](https://greentechlead.com/power/ge-vernova-vs-siemens-energy-vs-hitachi-energy-who-is-winning-the-2026-power-equipment-boom-55926)). Transformer lead times run to four years ([pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/)), Comfort Systems' backlog rose **81% to $12.45B** ([Nasdaq/Zacks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/can-comfort-systems-record-backlog-keep-demand-pipeline-strong)), and India's CG Power reported order intake **+90%** and revenue **+30%** in the September quarter ([Trade Brains](https://tradebrains.in/indian-markets/cg-power-q2-revenue-up-301-yoy-to-380154-cr-as-net-profit-surges-438-to-40909-cr-12741778)). The structural catch is that the power edge accrues to incumbents with existing plants and OEMs with backlog, not to new-build developers: Talen had to move its Amazon deal front-of-the-meter after FERC's rejection, and its Montour gas-plant rezoning was refused by county commissioners ([Power Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/talen-amazon-launch-18b-nuclear-ppa/); [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2026/2/talen-plans-pivot-after-pa-gas-plant-rezoning-proposal-fails-99122187)). Valuation already reflects a lot of this (GE Vernova ~75x trailing per Morningstar, Quanta ~63x trailing), and Vertiv's **-45% fall from its May record** shows what happens to second-derivative names whose multiples ran ahead of 2028 earnings ([Morningstar GEV](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/xnys/gev/quote); [Yahoo PWR](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PWR/); [Macrotrends VRT](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/VRT/vertiv-holdings/stock-price-history)).

The cleanest measured dividing line in all of software is pricing model. Every consumption-priced data platform re-accelerated: Snowflake's product revenue growth went 34% to **37%** across three quarters with the CFO citing "a meaningful step-up in AI revenue" and full-year guidance raised to $6.07B ([Snowflake Q2 FY27](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20260902386235/en/Snowflake-Reports-Financial-Results-for-the-Second-Quarter-of-Fiscal-2027)); Databricks hit **$6.9B annualised (+80%)** with its CEO saying "the agents are generating way more queries" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/16/databricks-revenue-growth-tops-80percent-to-6point9-billion-annualized.html)); MongoDB posted **30%** growth, its highest since FY24, with RPO +91% ([MongoDB IR](https://investors.mongodb.com/node/14616/pdf)); Palantir's US commercial revenue grew **149% to $764M** and full-year guidance was raised to +82% ([Palantir Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001321655/000132165526000039/a2026q2ex991pressrelease.htm)). Every seat-priced application vendor decelerated. The cost of the consumption edge is visible too: Snowflake guided gross margin down toward ~74% "to pay for AI" and Databricks reports shrinking margins as agents raise compute costs, so part of the uplift passes through to hyperscalers and model providers, though MongoDB's **900bp** of operating-margin expansion shows net capture is still positive ([SaaStr on Snowflake](https://www.saastr.com/5-interesting-learnings-from-snowflake-at-6-billion-in-revenue-37-growth-and-accelerating-126-nrr-and-a-gross-margin-guided-down-to-pay-for-ai/)). Coding is the one category where AI revenue is unambiguously large: Cursor went from $1B to more than **$4B** annualised in roughly seven months ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/134107-cursor-tops-4b-annualized-revenue/)), while the productivity evidence behind that spend is strikingly weak: METR's 2025 RCT found experienced developers **19% slower** with early-2025 tools while believing they were 20% faster, and its 2026 follow-up is statistically inconclusive ([METR summary](https://ingenire.com/blog/metr-2026-developer-productivity-study)). Buyers are paying for perceived speed and output volume, which is a durable willingness-to-pay as long as the perception holds, but it is the one top-tier edge where measured outcome and purchase behaviour point in different directions.

Ad platforms are the clearest winners in the information economy because they own the conversion data the models learn from. Meta's Q2 2026 showed **ad impressions +14% and average price per ad +12%**, the strongest evidence that AI targeting is raising auction yield rather than just volume; the **$75B Advantage+ run-rate** and the **+15.7% conversion** lift from its GEM models are company claims, not filed line items ([Meta 10-Q Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001326801/000162828026050705/meta-20260630.htm); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/416925/meta-boosts-advertising-in-q2-tries-to-reassure-i.html)). Google's Search & Other grew **+19% and +17%** in Q1 and Q2 2026 while holding >91% search share, so the measured answer to "is Google losing to ChatGPT" is no: ChatGPT is **0.21%** of site referrals against ~40% for Google, and Google externalises the cost of AI answers onto publishers through a **68% zero-click rate** ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-search-hits-63b-details-ai-mode-ad-tests/566613/); [Ahrefs via Milan Novotny](https://www.milan-novotny.com/statistics/chatgpt-vs-google-search-statistics/); [Blog Herald](https://blogherald.com/seo/n-zero-click-google-searches-ai-overviews-2026/)). AppLovin's Q2 shows the flip side of a model-driven edge: **+53%** revenue but a 20% stock drop on "lighter-than-expected model improvements," and the stock is **-59% YTD** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/applovin-corp-app-q2-2026-010639084.html); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/applovin-stock-near-52week-low-fundamentals-vs-bearish-trend-93CH-4954022)).

Among buyers of AI, the quantified evidence clusters in regulated, data-rich, oligopolistic industries. JPMorgan says it spends **~$2B a year on AI and saves about the same**, with **~150,000** employees using its internal LLM weekly and "discrete areas where we did reduce jobs by 30% or 40%" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-07/jpmorgan-s-dimon-says-ai-cost-savings-now-matching-money-spent); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/jpmorgan-to-hire-more-ai-specialists-fewer-bankers-dimon-tells-bloomberg-93CH-4702943)); Bank of America's reported **~$800M of benefits on ~$400M of cost** is secondary and unverified ([Plus AI](https://plusai.com/blog/ai-in-financial-services-real-roi-data/)). Lemonade is the only insurer with a claims-cycle number: gross loss ratio **60% versus 67%**, a record **5%** loss-adjustment-expense ratio and more than half of claims automated, which the market ignored, sending the stock down 24% on the print ([Lemonade Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001691421/000169142126000047/lmndshareholderletterq22.htm)). The structural reason this edge is retained rather than competed away is market structure: Morgan Stanley's P&C model has only **~10% of $6.0B of gross AI savings** reaching operating earnings because competition passes the rest to customers, so the AI dividend sticks only where pricing is sticky ([Actuary.info summary](https://actuary.info/insights/morgan-stanley-ai-savings-forecast-pc-insurers)). Klarna is the cautionary case: revenue per employee **$1.24M** and headcount **-49%** versus 2022, then a partial rehiring of humans for service quality ([Klarna Q4 2025](https://s205.q4cdn.com/644747736/files/doc_earnings/2025/q4/earnings-result/Q4-25-Klarna-Group-plc-Press-Release.pdf)).

In the physical world the edge is narrow but real where volume is disclosed: Symbotic is profitable on a **$22.5B backlog** (with ~85% Walmart concentration) ([Symbotic 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001837240/000183724026000042/q3268-k_ex991.htm)); Intuitive's procedures grew **~15%** with 546 placements versus 427 ([Intuitive 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001035267/000103526726000071/isrg-20260930.htm)); Waymo reached **500,000 paid rides a week**, tenfold in two years, though it publishes no unit economics, Alphabet's Other Bets lost **$1.8B** in Q2, and Waymo has not updated its weekly figure since spring, which is itself a signal ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/27/waymo-skyrocketing-ridership-in-one-chart/); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)). Tesla's FSD is a measurable P&L line (**~1.74M** active subscriptions, >75% attach on new North American deliveries) even though its unsupervised Cybercab service is one city and ~2M cumulative miles ([Tesla 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026066738/exhibit9914.htm); [Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/4867/tesla-q3-2026-earnings-call-recap-everything-you-need-to-know)). Defense is the fastest-growing measured revenue in physical AI (Palantir **+93%**, AeroVironment **+141%** partly via acquisition), with the caveat that the headline "$10B" and "$20B" Army agreements are ceilings, not obligations ([Palantir 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001321655/000132165526000041/pltr-20260630.htm); [Govly](https://app.govly.com/public/signals/125307)).

The last edge is the one the market has most clearly mispriced relative to reported numbers. Thomson Reuters' Legal segment grew **10% organically** and the company raised its full-year outlook ([TR Q2 2026](https://ir.thomsonreuters.com/news-releases/news-release-details/thomson-reuters-reports-second-quarter-2026-results)); RELX Legal grew **10%** ([City AM](https://www.cityam.com/relx-defies-ai-disruption-fears-as-growth-holds-firm/)); MSCI's index revenue grew **~18%** ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/msci-q3-earnings-surpass-estimates-134200359.html)); and law-firm revenue grew **~12%** with billable hours up **4%**, meaning Harvey's growth to a reported **>$400M ARR** is additive spend, not fee rebate, so far ([Best Law Firms](https://www.bestlawfirms.com/articles/ai-billable-hour-law-firm-revenue-2026/8427); [SaaSrise](https://www.saasrise.com/deals/harvey-raises-550m-in-funding-round-valuation-reaches-155b-fdaacbc6-e5ae-49f1-a9e7-224789dfde95)). Yet RELX fell **11-14% in a day** when Anthropic shipped a legal plugin, Wolters Kluwer is **~23% below its high** with Morningstar cutting its moat rating to narrow, and S&P Global trades at **~19.9x forward, below its peer average** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ai-tool-release-sends-legal-134708494.html); [Morningstar WKL](https://www.morningstar.com/stocks/xams/wkl/quote); [Yahoo on SPGI](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/p-global-spgi-benefiting-ai-144615963)). The structural case is citable primary content, editorial enhancements and regulatory need for authoritative citation; the honest counter is that no one yet knows whether 10% organic growth survives frontier labs shipping vertical agents with retrieval over primary law, and the incumbents' own response, becoming the data pipe inside the LLM via MCP connectors, trades UI lock-in for distribution ([LSEG Q1 2026](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/2026/london-stock-exchange-group-plc-q1-2026-trading-update)). This is the edge with the thinnest evidence on durability and the widest gap between numbers and price.

## Where AI destroys value: anything priced by the seat, the hour or the click

The losers are not where the February 2026 panic put them. The clearest measured casualties share one trait: they sold low-complexity, text-in-text-out output to consumers or cost-centre buyers by the unit. Chegg's revenue fell **48%** in Q1 and **51%** in Q2 2026 after it cut 45% of staff, and its antitrust suit against Google over AI Overviews was dismissed on the reasoning that an expectation of referral traffic is not an agreement ([StockTitan 10-Q summary](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CHGG/10-q-chegg-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-a76e084cb71a.html); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3824124/the-top-5-analyst-questions-from-cheggs-q2-earnings-call); [Relve](https://relvehq.com/blog/noise/google-ai-overviews-suits-dismissed)). Stack Overflow's new questions are down **~99%** from their 2014 peak ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/stack-overflow-drops-to-1-442-questions-in-july-down-99-from-2014-peak/)). Getty Images, after its Shutterstock merger collapsed and iStock traffic fell because "search platforms implement AI-generated answers," **filed for Chapter 11 on 21 October 2026** ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/getty-images-holdings-inc-gety-050301082.html); [SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1898496/000121390026073816/ea0296572-8k425_getty.htm)). Fiverr's writing and translation category fell **more than 24%** on a trailing basis with active buyers down ~22%, and Upwork's gross services volume fell **3.6%** with a ~24% workforce cut ([Investing.com Fiverr transcript](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-fiverr-q2-2026-miss-and-outlook-spark-sharp-selloff-93CH-4820585); [Gardiner Colin on Upwork](https://www.gardinercolin.com/p/ai-is-eating-upwork-from-the-bottom)). Intuit is the large-cap version: Goldman downgraded it to Sell on the arithmetic that an AI system processes a standard return for **~$0.12 against TurboTax's ~$162 of revenue per return**, the company cut TurboTax guidance and ~17% of its workforce, and the stock is roughly **half** its peak ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/goldman-sachs-downgrades-intuit-ai-104654463.html); [InvestingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/why-is-intuit-stock-crashing-also-after-its-earnings-last-night-20260521/)). The structural reason in every case is the same: no proprietary data, a Google-dependent funnel, and a product a free model can produce.

Labour sold by the hour is next, and here deflation is now quantified by the sellers themselves. TCS says **~50% of active engagements** have been AI-repriced with contract values down **10-20% on average** on those projects, which implies a mid-single-digit annual revenue headwind and matches the reported 0.6-2.8% growth at the Indian majors; Infosys cut FY27 guidance twice to **1-2%** even as AI-related revenue rose to **10.5%** of the total ([rutviz on Indian IT](https://rutviz.substack.com/p/indian-it-a-few-months-later-and); [BigGo on Infosys](https://finance.biggo.com/news/c178960e-dfbd-446e-83d0-656f2f5a002a)). Cognizant's Q2 bookings fell **6%** even as fixed-price and outcome-based contracts crossed half of revenue, and the stock was -36% YTD ([Cognizant Q2](https://investors.cognizant.com/news-and-events/news/news-details/2026/Cognizant-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx); [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/cognizant-has-fallen-36-in-2026-could-the-ai-forum-change-the-story)). Accenture is the relative winner, and its trajectory shows what "priced in" looks like: a record **~20% one-day drop** in June on a bookings miss, then a **+20%** day on 1 October on **$22.2B** of Q4 bookings and an FY27 guide of only **+3-6%** local-currency growth, low single digits for a firm that grew double digits in 2021-22 ([Sherwood](https://sherwood.news/markets/accenture-tumbles-after-q3-new-bookings-and-q4-revenue-guidance-disappoint/); [Accenture 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001467373/000146737326000037/q4fy26earnings8-kexhibit.htm)). Call-centre BPO is where "deflation in billing" has become actual negative revenue: Concentrix revenue fell **1.2%** in its August quarter with a **$1.05B goodwill impairment** after a guidance cut in which it said some clients were withdrawing CX outsourcing entirely to fund AI; RBC called the sub-sector "uninvestable"; TaskUs's EBITDA margin fell **220bp** as one client automated moderation work ([Concentrix 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001803599/000180359926000155/cnxc-20260831.htm); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-06-30/call-center-stocks-fall-on-worry-ai-is-makes-them-uninvestible); [TaskUs Q1](https://ir.taskus.com/news-releases/news-release-details/taskus-announces-fiscal-first-quarter-2026-results)). Consulting and research subscriptions show the same pattern in softer form: McKinsey has cut roughly **10%** of staff, and Gartner's contract value grew only **1.7%** year on year against a historical 8-10%, the cleanest "LLM substitutes for research" signal in the dataset ([Outsource Accelerator](https://news.outsourceaccelerator.com/mckinsey-layoffs-ai-consulting/); [Gartner 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000749251/000074925126000243/it-06302026xex991.htm)).

Intermediaries without data are squeezed from both sides. The Trade Desk guided to its first-ever revenue decline, fell 22% on the day and was **-68% YTD** by 9 October, with UBS citing share leakage toward Amazon's aggressively priced DSP ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/trade-desk-stock-down-68-095103537.html); [Yahoo on UBS](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/trade-desk-vs-amazon-ad-155400566.html)). WPP's H1 like-for-like revenue fell **4.7%** and its CEO said AI brings "some short-term deflationary impact on pricing" because clients expect productivity gains passed through, while Goldman forecasts negative agency organic growth through 2026 ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/wpp-is-on-track-with-turnaround-plan-as-revenue-drops-56-in-first-half-of-2026/); [Marketing Dive](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/wpp-losses-intensify-as-hopes-turn-to-ai-golden-age-for-marketing/804500/); [Investing.com](https://za.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/ad-agency-sector-outlook-publicis-leads-as-wpp-retreats-and-omnicom-integrates-ipg-93CH-4467345)). Omnicom's +7.2% organic growth is a consolidation and synergy story, not an AI uplift ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/omnicom-q3-2026-slides-organic-growth-hits-72-margins-expand-93CH-4967706)). Publishers are the silent loser: search referrals fell **~33% globally and 38% in the US** in the year to November 2025, and licensing is an order of magnitude too small to offset it (Reddit's data licensing is **$43M a quarter**, ~5% of revenue, with its ~$60M-a-year Google deal expiring in H1 2027) ([DesignRush on Reuters Institute](https://news.designrush.com/ai-overviews-publisher-traffic-website-strategy-2026); [Yahoo on Reddit](https://finance.yahoo.com/media-advertising/articles/reddits-data-licensing-revenue-grew-125000998.html)). Inside the AI stack itself the losers are the integrators: Super Micro at **9.3%** gross margin with a Goldman Sell, HPE server margins down from 10.8% to 6.4% ([Fintool](https://fintool.com/news/goldman-smci-sell-margin-erosion); [iTiger](https://www.itiger.com/news/1156934151)).

Seat-based SaaS is the category where the market has run ahead of the evidence. The measured facts are deceleration, not collapse: Salesforce grew **11%**, Workday subscription **13.9%** with an FY28 preview of ~11%, Adobe **13%**, and their "AI ARR" lines (Agentforce **$1.5B**, Workday **~$600M**, Adobe **>$500-650M**) are growing 150-240% but amount to 3-6% of revenue and have not offset core deceleration ([Salesforce release](https://www.salesforce.com/news/press-releases/2026/08/26/fy27-q2-earnings/); [Workday release](https://en-ca.newsroom.workday.com/2026-08-27-Workday-Announces-Fiscal-2027-Second-Quarter-Financial-Results); [247WallSt on Adobe](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/02/the-adobe-stock-debate-is-about-to-get-interesting/)). Salesforce changed its Agentforce definition to include Slackbot and Headless 360 and is moving to less frequent AI-ARR reporting, which is a yellow flag on organic growth ([Vantagepoint](https://vantagepoint.io/blog/sf/salesforce-q2-fy27-earnings-record-quarter)). The most concrete seat compression so far is the vendors cutting their own seats: Salesforce support from **9,000 to ~5,000** with agents handling half of interactions, ServiceNow not backfilling open jobs, GitLab cutting 14% ([Fox/AP](https://www.fox4news.com/news/salesforce-ceo-cuts-4000-jobs-ai-agents-handle-half-customer-work); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/22/servicenow-mcdermott-ai-jobs-earnings.html)). Atlassian's FQ3 (+32% revenue, "continued strong seat expansion in Jira," stock +29% in a day) is the one direct contradiction of the seat-compression thesis ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/01/atlassian-team-q3-stock-2026-earnings.html)). On a market level, JPMorgan called February's drawdown the largest non-recessionary 12-month software selloff in more than 30 years, and the median public software company now trades at **~4.5-4.8x NTM revenue**, near 2022-23 trough levels, with Adobe at **~10x** and Salesforce at **~14.5x** forward earnings ([Dunham](https://dunham.com/insights/saas-pocalypse-ai-crushing-software-stocks); [Clouded Judgement 22 Oct 2026](https://cloudedjudgement.substack.com/p/clouded-judgement-102226-ai-goes); [Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/better-buy/2026/10/23/adobe-vs-salesforce-which-technology-stock-is-a-better-buy-in-2026/)). The market is pricing seat SaaS as if it were BPO; the reported numbers say it is a decelerating business with a pricing-model transition underway, which makes it a candidate for the contrarian sleeve rather than the short book, with the explicit caveat that every print that shows re-acceleration (Atlassian) moves the stock 20-30% in a day and every print that does not keeps it cheap.

Two false positives deserve flagging because they appear on every "AI loser" screen: CoStar (**-49% YTD**) and Boston Scientific (-54%) were among the ten S&P 500 stocks down more than 40% by late July, and the source itself cautions those declines were company-specific, not AI ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/10-stocks-lost-over-40-181945525.html)). Conversely, Teleperformance sits at its **52-week high** on a ~10x P/E despite shrinking revenue, so even inside the "uninvestable" BPO group the market has found a floor ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/corporate-news/teleperformance-stock-at-eur-78-36-teleperformance-stock-rises-3-13-percent/70384952)).

## Narrative without numbers: where the AI story outruns the evidence

A long list of widely traded AI stories has no measured outcome behind it as of October 2026, and a rebuild should treat them as optionality to be sized small or avoided, not as edges. Humanoid robotics is the purest case. Tesla's Optimus has **zero external sales**, is built "primarily for learning and data collection," and Musk says it is "at least a year, maybe a little longer" from a meaningful production rate ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/09/25/tesla-optimus-production-ramp-hands-ai-generalization-problems/)). Figure's BMW deployment is a logistics pilot with an unverified 40-unit claim ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/bmw-group-deploys-figure-03-humanoid-after-tests-previous-version/)). The entire public humanoid revenue base is essentially Unitree's **~$129M** of 2025 humanoid sales, with ex-items profit **down 52.6%** in Q1 2026, yet its IPO was reportedly **>8,000x** retail oversubscribed and dragged Teradyne (robotics 7.5% of revenue) and Symbotic along in sympathy ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/06/chinese-humanoid-robot-maker-unitree-prices-ipo-at-9-billion-valuation.html); [24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/11/robotics-stocks-rally-as-unitrees-8000x-oversubscribed-ipo-draws-frenzied-demand-ouster-symbotic-and-teradyne-in-focus/)). Harmonic Drive, the key actuator supplier, guides its humanoid ramp to FY2027 ([ad-hoc-news](https://www.ad-hoc-news.de/boerse/news/nebenwerte/harmonic-drive-presents-actuator-designs-as-robot-demand-builds-for/70407077)). Industrial robot demand is in an ordinary upcycle (IFR installs **+11%** in 2025, forecast +9% in 2026), not a step change ([IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally)).

Healthcare AI is adoption without ROI. Ambient scribes are the fastest-adopted technology in healthcare, but the best multi-site study shows **13-16 minutes a day** of saved documentation time, a UCLA RCT found burnout improvement with no statistically significant time saving, and the Peterson Health Technology Institute concluded scribes "have not proven a financial return on investment" ([Commure summary of JAMA study](https://www.commure.com/blog-scribe/dax-ai-scribe); [PHTI](https://www.phti.org/announcement/ai-scribes-reduce-clinician-burnout/)). Epic's native AI Charting compresses standalone-vendor pricing, so value accrues to the private EHR incumbent and Microsoft rather than any listed pure-play ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/02/05/epic-releases-ai-charting-ambient-ai-market-implications/)). Insurer AI is 99% administrative at UnitedHealth and politically exposed through the nH Predict litigation and prior-authorisation rollbacks ([Spokesman-Review/Bloomberg](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/jun/19/unitedhealths-3-billion-ai-push-has-bots-calling-d/)). Industrial AI at Siemens, Honeywell and Emerson is vendor case studies with no company-level financial disclosure ([Emerj](https://emerj.com/artificial-intelligence-at-siemens/)). Visa and Mastercard's agentic commerce is 2026 product launches with no measured fraud or revenue outcome ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/payments/news/mastercard-visa-launch-agentic-ai-risk-tools)). Fast-food voice AI handles **13%** of Wendy's drive-thru orders, barely changed from 12% a year earlier, with worse guest experience ([MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/food-beverage/thank-yous-at-the-drive-thru-window-drop-239-points-when-ai-takes-the-order)).

Three further patterns separate narrative from measurement. First, AI-attributed layoffs look partly performative: Challenger's share of announced cuts citing AI spiked to **40% in May 2026** and then fell to **9%** by September, with Challenger itself noting that citing AI "can win over investors" ([Challenger May 2026](https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Challenger-Report-May-2026.pdf); [analysis of Sept data](https://alicantetechvibes.com/research-labs/ai-layoffs-job-cuts-2026/)). The Yale Budget Lab finds no clear aggregate labour-market disruption through August 2026, while Stanford/ADP's "Canaries" update shows the one well-identified effect, 22-25-year-olds in AI-exposed jobs **~19% below** peers via reduced hiring rather than layoffs ([Yale Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/sites/default/files/page_to_pdf/1419/publication_1419.pdf); [Stanford DEL](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)). Second, management claims that contradict peers' measurements should be discounted: Globant says it sees no deflationary pressure while TCS, the largest player, quantifies 10-20% repricing ([Yahoo Globant](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/globant-q2-2026-earnings-call-123000206.html)). Third, the China AI stack has revenue follow-through with cash divergence: Cambricon's revenue rose **108%** while operating cash flow fell **66%**, on a ~**214x** trailing multiple and dependence on SMIC 7nm yields around 20%; Alibaba's June-quarter profit fell **75%** on AI spend before a **$10.2B** dilutive placement, and Alibaba and Tencent lost **$66B** in a day in March when results lacked a monetisation plan ([Implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/cambricon-h1-revenue-doubles-china-ai-chips/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/24/alibaba-share-placement-drop-ai-hong-kong.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-20/alibaba-tencent-shed-66-billion-after-ai-vision-falls-flat)). SEC enforcement offers no help: 2026 AI-washing cases are micro-cap frauds settled for tens of thousands of dollars, and no large-cap action or 2026 short-seller report on a listed AI name surfaced ([law-firm alert summary](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/sec-ai-washing-enforcement-three-cases-2026/)).

## Fragility: the cycle has moved from equity-funded to credit-funded, and that changes who loses

The bubble debate has shifted from "is there demand?" (answered yes by backlogs, Nvidia's doubling, and GPU rental prices rebounding ~40% on one-year H100 contracts between October 2025 and March 2026) to "is the financing sound?" ([GPUsmith on SemiAnalysis index](https://gpusmith.com/articles/en/h100-rental-price-history-trends)). Big-four 2026 capex guidance stepped up again in July to roughly **$710-730B** against ~$410B spent in 2025, with Alphabet guiding 2027 "significantly" higher and Goldman projecting **~$1.14T** for 2027 ([Analysis Atlas](https://analysis-atlas.com/research/hyperscaler-ai-capex-2026/); [IO Fund](https://io-fund.com/ai-stocks/ai-capex-1-trillion-estimates-too-low)). The qualitative break in 2026 is that the cycle is no longer self-funded at its most cash-generative names: free cash flow turned negative at Alphabet (**~-$5.9B**) and Amazon (**~-$7.6B**) in Q2, and Meta's Q3 showed FCF of **~-$2.1B** with a capex guide of $135-145B, a print the stock fell 7-10% on ([Fierce Network](https://www.fierce-network.com/cloud/google-cloud-surges-alphabets-ai-capex-problem-grows); [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/us-stocks/amazon-q2-earnings-results-why-amzn-stock-jumped-analysis); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/21/meta-q3-earnings-mark-zuckerberg-capex-muse/)). Five large US cloud names sold **~$274B of investment-grade bonds** in 2026 through late September versus ~$30B a year in 2020-24, hyperscalers rose to **~10% of global IG issuance** from under 1% in 2024, Meta's Hyperion SPV placed **$27B** of 2049 bonds, and Moody's reportedly counts **~$662B** of signed-but-not-commenced data-center leases ([Marcus Today](https://marcustoday.com.au/2026/10/who-funds-ai-debt-and-who-is-stretched/); [Tom's Hardware on IMF GFSR](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/the-imf-highlights-how-a-financial-wipeout-in-the-ai-sector-could-become-a-global-contagion-agency-report-shines-light-on-circular-economics-and-leveraged-loans); [Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/when-will-the-ai-bubble-burst)). The IMF's October 2026 Global Financial Stability Report names an AI-equity and credit correction one of three salient near-term risks, having analysed 14 circular transactions worth ~$300B of equity and ~$650B of debt exchanged for ~$1.4T of future revenue commitments ([Quartz](https://qz.com/imf-ai-stock-crash-global-financial-stability-101326); [Tech Times](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/328970/20261014/imf-names-14t-ai-financing-loop-top-trigger-global-market-shock.htm)).

Stress is already visible at the leveraged margin, not yet at the core. CoreWeave carries **~$51.6B** of debt and lease obligations, its 2031 notes yield **~15-16%**, credit-default swaps implied a **~63%** five-year default probability, and the stock fell 37% in October to a new low as a $4B CoreWeave-anchored data-center loan reportedly struggled to syndicate ([CoreWeave 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001769628/000176962826000357/ex991pr.htm); [Trefis](https://www.trefis.com/stock/crwv/articles2/618845/what-sent-coreweave-stock-lower/2026-10-15); [Marcus Today](https://marcustoday.com.au/2026/10/who-funds-ai-debt-and-who-is-stretched/)). Oracle is the largest investment-grade AI balance sheet: **$664B** of remaining performance obligations, roughly half OpenAI-related, FY26 free cash flow of **-$23.7B**, a ~$40B financing plan against $70B+ of capex, a July downgrade to **BBB-**, record CDS near 203bp, and a stock **-63% from its high** ([Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/13/oracle-fy2027-q1-earnings-analysis); [AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/oracle-nvidia-alphabet-spacex-cds-hit-records-on-ai-debt); [Macrotrends ORCL](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/charts/ORCL/oracle/stock-price-history)). The counterparty behind both is OpenAI, whose headline $1.4T of commitments were reportedly reset toward ~$600B by 2030 and whose run-rate reports (~$50B versus earlier $68-70B figures) triggered the 8 October selloff; those claims come from aggregators and are unverified, which is itself a fragility, since the market is repricing on reports about a private company ([Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5209792); [Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/analytics/traders-blog/openai-revenue-ai-selloff-october-2026)). The accounting debate adds a slow-burning risk: Michael Burry's estimate that 5-6 year depreciation lives on chips he deems realistic at ~2.5 years overstate hyperscaler profits by **~$176B** over 2026-28 (Nvidia disputes the math), Morgan Stanley's view that up to **40%** of 2026 capex will not be operational until 2027-29, and the mechanical point that GDP measures flows, so with AI capex contributing roughly a third of 2026 US growth a mere plateau subtracts from growth ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/10/62580225/a-1-trillion-depreciation-bomb-is-ticking-beneath-the-ai-market); [Veritas Europaea](https://www.veritaseuropaea.eu/blog/2026/09/15/ai-vs-us-economy-growth-2026/)). Index concentration turns this into everyone's problem: Goldman has the seven largest stocks supplying **46%** of 2026 S&P 500 earnings growth, so a cap-weighted index fund is an implicit AI-capex position ([24/7 Wall St on Goldman](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/13/goldman-sachs-says-ai-is-driving-half-of-sp-500-earnings-growth-that-number-cuts-both-ways/)).

Two further fragilities are structural rather than financial. Local opposition to data centers is accelerating: US projects cancelled on local opposition went from 2 in 2023 to **25 in 2025**, at least **75 projects worth ~$130B** were blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 alone, moratoria exist in Denver, Oklahoma City and ~20 Michigan communities, and Gallup finds **7 in 10** Americans would oppose a nearby AI data center ([Uptime Intelligence](https://intelligence.uptimeinstitute.com/resource/data-center-cancellations-rise-public-opposition-grows); [Civic Media](https://civicmedia.us/news/2026/05/29/more-cities-are-pressing-pause-on-data-centers-as-local-backlash-grows)). That favours incumbents with secured sites and power and penalises neoclouds and late builders. And model-layer price deflation keeps compounding: inference prices fell **~43% in ten weeks** to August 2026, OpenAI cut flagship pricing 80%, open-weight models reportedly took **38%** of enterprise token volume in Q1, and the "SaaSpocalypse" itself was a demonstration that a single product launch from a frontier lab can erase ~$285B of software value in 48 hours ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3363549/enterprise-ai-costs-hit-2026-low-driven-price-wars-chinese-open-source-models-research); [N47](https://www.n47.com/insights/the-ai-price-collapse-has-only-just-begun); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/donmuir/2026/02/04/300-billion-evaporated-the-saaspocalypse-has-begun/)). The historical analogues (railways, 1999-2002 fiber) agree that the technology endures while early infrastructure equity holders are frequently wiped out and users of the cheapened input win; the important difference is that GPUs depreciate in three to six years, so an overbuild does not leave a decades-long cheap asset the way dark fiber did ([Derek Thompson](https://www.derekthompson.org/p/yes-ai-is-a-bubble-there-is-no-question); [Francis Okafor](https://www.francisokafor.com/insights/is-ai-a-bubble-engineer-assessment-capex-depreciation)). Policy risk, by contrast, has loosened: the EU delayed AI Act high-risk obligations to **December 2027**, US chip policy turned an export ban into a **25% Section 232 toll** on H200 sales to China, and the Bartz v. Anthropic settlement priced pirated training data at ~$3,000 a work while leaving lawful-acquisition training as fair use, a cost rather than an existential risk for well-capitalised labs ([Sidley](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/22/eu-lawmakers-reach-provisional-agreement-to-delay-key-eu-ai-act-obligations/); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/14/trump-tariff-nvidia-chips-china); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/judge-approves-record-1-5-billion-settlement-involving-anthropic/)).

## Shaping a portfolio around the edges: own the toll booths and the buyers, rent the hypergrowth, hedge the capex

For an individual investor rebuilding an equity portfolio around this evidence, the structural logic is more important than any ticker list, because the edges are defined by business model and the tickers will change. The evidence supports five sleeves plus a hedge, and it supports sizing them by evidence quality and valuation rather than by narrative excitement. The first and largest sleeve is volume-priced scarce complements at ordinary multiples: the leading GPU, foundry, lithography and memory names trade at **~5-25x forward earnings** with exceptional measured earnings, and they monetise token volume without per-token price exposure, which is the Jevons position. The discipline required here is to accept cyclicality and crowding risk explicitly, size memory smaller than logic because its pricing power is newest and most cyclical (SK hynix fell 31% in two weeks on CXMT progress and rate fears while still up 97% on the year), and avoid leveraged single-stock products entirely ([Yahoo on CXMT](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/samsung-sk-hynix-shares-drop-053408695.html)). Non-US exposure belongs here too, in the Taiwan supply chain with monthly-verifiable revenue (TSMC at ~21x forward is cheaper than most of its customers), while the Indian grid names (CG Power ~111x trailing) have the follow-through but not the price and the Chinese internet names (Tencent ~14x) have the price but not the monetisation ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/10/23/this-company-behind-the-worlds-ai-chips-could-be-a-huge-winner/); [Univest](https://univest.in/blogs/why-schneider-electric-infrastructure-ltd-share-price-rise-today-2026); [Yahoo TCEHY](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TCEHY/)).

The second sleeve is moated buyers of AI: the companies the Goldman "AI productivity beneficiary" basket was built around, high labour-cost firms that adopt rather than sell AI, filtered by the Morgan Stanley pass-through insight so that only those with proprietary data and sticky pricing qualify ([iTiger on Goldman basket](https://www.itiger.com/news/1107581028)). The measured evidence points to the largest banks, scaled insurers with disclosed claims automation, the first-party-data ad platforms at 17-23x forward earnings, and, more speculatively, the de-rated legal, tax and financial data incumbents whose ~10% organic growth has not yet been dented and whose multiples now sit below peers. That last group is the contrarian sleeve and should be sized as such: the evidence on durability is thin, the February selloff showed how violently the market punishes them on a product launch, and the bull case rests on an untested assumption that citable primary content and regulatory need survive vertical agents. Seat-based SaaS at ~10-15x forward earnings belongs in the same contrarian bucket for the names with a real pricing-model transition underway (ServiceNow's 50% non-seat net-new business is the best disclosed example), not for those whose only answer is a redefined "AI ARR" line ([ServiceNow Q1 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/servicenow-inc-q1-2026-earnings-001811104.html)). CROs and life-science tools fit here as a funding-cycle long with free AI optionality, sized for the cycle, with Veeva and the monetising platforms as the AI-specific expression.

The third sleeve is consumption-priced hypergrowth, which the evidence says is the best measured edge in software and the most expensive asset class in the market. The honest position for an individual investor is to own it small, to prefer the names whose multiples have already compressed (Snowflake at ~19-21x sales with accelerating growth, MongoDB at ~45-56x forward earnings and -15% YTD, Datadog) over the ones at 50-80x sales, and to treat Palantir and CrowdStrike as positions whose future returns depend on multiples staying extreme. Power and grid equipment is a fourth, smaller sleeve with the same logic: the backlog evidence is the hardest in the whole dataset, but GE Vernova at ~75x trailing and Quanta at ~63x trailing already pay for years of it, so the entry discipline matters more than the thesis; Vertiv's -45% and Vistra's -31% drawdowns from highs show the group does offer entry points, and the incumbent nuclear and gas IPPs at ~15-21x forward (Vistra, Constellation) are the cheaper expression of the same constraint ([Fool on CEG/VST/GEV](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/24/constellation-vistra-and-ge-vernova-ranked-from-wo/)). A fifth sleeve, physical automation with disclosed volume (warehouse systems, surgical robotics, Alphabet for Waymo), is a long-duration option that has already repriced (Symbotic -27% YTD, Intuitive -29% from high) and should exclude the humanoid-sympathy names outright.

The avoid-or-short list follows directly from the measured losers and the fragility map, and for an individual investor "avoid" is the practical form of most of it, since shorting carries borrow cost, unlimited loss and the risk that even confirmed losers bounce (Teleperformance at a 52-week high). The strongest evidence-backed shorts or avoids are: junk-financed neocloud equity and Oracle credit, where the tell is bond yields and CDS rather than the stock; call-centre BPO and gig platforms for commodity text work; server and ODM integrators whose gross margins are single digits while revenue doubles; consumer Q&A and search-funnel businesses; ad intermediaries without first-party data; and humanoid and quantum names trading on pre-revenue narrative (IonQ 53% below its high after a $2B equity raise; NuScale and Oklo down 55% and 39% from highs) ([HeyGoTrade on IonQ](https://www.heygotrade.com/en/news/ionq-ionq-stock-news-october-16-2026/); [Benzinga on nuclear](https://benzinga.com/markets/tech/25/11/49061360/nuclear-stock-meltdown-continues-for-oklo-nuscale-nano)). Crowded longs to trim rather than short are the ones where 2026 gains are concentrated and multiples are extreme: Korean memory after a doubling, security platforms at 200x, Palantir at 80x sales, and the entire set of 2x single-stock ETFs. Two categories should not be shorted despite the narrative: seat-SaaS, which is already at trough multiples and moves 20-30% on any re-acceleration print, and IT services, where Accenture's +20% day on record bookings shows the deflation thesis is known and the bookings cycle still dominates.

The hedge is the part most individual investors skip. Because the seven largest stocks supply roughly 46% of index earnings growth and AI capex is roughly a third of US GDP growth, a conventional cap-weighted core already carries large AI-capex beta, so an edge-based rebuild should either reduce the cap-weighted core or treat the infrastructure sleeve as partly a substitute for it rather than an addition. Within the AI exposure, the credit-cycle lesson from 1999-2001 is that losses migrate from equity to lenders at exactly this stage, so the practical hedges are discrimination by balance sheet (own the self-funding hyperscalers and suppliers, not the debt-funded builders), a tilt toward buyers of the deflating input rather than sellers of it, and exposure to complements (power, memory, networking) that retain pricing even if GPU capex plateaus. The IMF's downside scenario of a 1.1% hit to global GDP by 2028 from a steep drop in AI investment is a reminder that cyclical and rate-sensitive assets outside tech carry hidden AI beta too ([Moneywise on IMF](https://moneywise.com/news/economy/imf-ai-debt-leverage-data-centers-2026)). A rebuild that ends up with most of its risk in three semiconductor names and a software index has not diversified across the edges; it has concentrated in one of them.

## Conclusion

The most useful change in understanding from this evidence is that "AI winner" and "AI loser" are not industry labels but pricing-model and data-ownership labels, and that the market worked this out in the first half of 2026 faster than most narratives did. The real dividing line runs between businesses that monetise volume or scarcity (compute, power, consumption-priced data, first-party-data ads) and businesses that monetise units of human output (seats, hours, clicks, returns, call minutes); the former re-accelerated and re-rated, the latter deflated and de-rated, and the companies most loudly "AI-first" in their messaging (Chegg, Concentrix, Salesforce's redefined Agentforce) are disproportionately on the losing side. That makes the stated prior instructive in a different way than intended: CROs look bullish because the biotech funding cycle turned, not because of AI, and the lesson generalises, since much of what is attributed to AI in 2026 earnings is cycle, consolidation or cost-cutting wearing an AI label.

For an investor rebuilding around these edges, the implication is that the edge itself is now mostly a valuation and financing question rather than a discovery question. The measured winners are known and expensive; the measured losers are known and in several cases already cheap; the mispricings sit in the middle, in de-rated incumbents whose numbers have not yet broken and in cyclical AI beneficiaries nobody is pricing for AI. The asymmetry that remains is on the financing side: the cycle has shifted from equity-funded to debt- and SPV-funded, stress has surfaced in credit before equity at every step this year, and a cap-weighted index already carries a large hidden position in the continuation of hyperscaler capex. The portfolio that respects the evidence therefore owns the toll booths at ordinary multiples, owns the moated buyers who keep the deflation dividend, rents the hypergrowth in small size, treats the contrarian incumbents as a sized bet on durability that the data does not yet settle, and spends real effort on hedging the capex bet it did not know it already had.
