# Non-consensus addendum: 22 survivors from 136 tested

*Addendum to "AI Value Flows to Volume, Not Seats". Research, not personalised advice. Prices as of 23 Oct 2026 unless stated. Two rounds merged: round 1 (40 tested, 2 survived) and round 2 (96 tested, 20 survived).*

## Method

The first report was judged "way too consensus", so this pass started from the opposite end. Bottom-up, we took measured AI usage (which tasks are actually being automated, by occupation, from the Anthropic Economic Index and the OpenAI usage paper) and asked which listed company sells that task by the unit, or buys it as a cost line. Top-down, we ran six contrarian classes against the same data: bottleneck shift (the cheapened input moves the constraint somewhere else), over-punished (sold off on an AI story the filings contradict), tail supplier (an unlabelled input to the buildout), complement (demand for the thing AI cannot do), demand creation (Jevons: cheaper output, more of it), and non-US (the same mechanism on a market where nobody has written it up). That produced roughly 130 candidates across the two rounds.

Every candidate went through two independent refuters. The consensus refuter asked whether the name sits in a Goldman, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, UBS or Citi AI basket, how many 2026 sell-side or press pieces frame it as an AI play, and whether it is up 60%+ year-to-date on that story (or down 40%+ on a published loser story). The evidence refuter asked whether the numbers exist at the cited primary source and whether that source attributes them to AI or to something else. The default was strict: loose attribution meant refuted, and uncertainty went against the candidate. Across 136 tests (a few names were run twice under different framings), 22 survived both lenses. Web-search budget ran out during round 2, so bank-basket membership was checked by direct page fetches only; where that leaves a gap the entry says so.

## The edges

Ranked best first across both rounds by evidence quality times non-consensus times clarity of mechanism, using the refuters' confidence scores as the first input and their caveats as the second. Grouped by class, but the number is the global rank. Five edges are "avoid" or datapoints rather than positions; they are ranked on the same scale because a measured negative is worth as much as a measured positive. One class is added to the six: the bottom-up pass surfaced buyers and unit-sellers of the automated task, which fit none of the top-down labels, so they get their own heading.

### Bottom-up: buyers and unit-sellers of the task

**1. Airbnb (NASDAQ: ABNB). Long. Support cost per booking down 16% year-on-year, in the filing, and the stock is priced as a travel name.**

Customer support is a direct per-booking cost for a marketplace: cancellations, refunds, host-guest disputes. Airbnb's in-house assistant now closes a large share of contacts without a human, so the cost per booking falls while bookings grow, and the saving lands in EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) margin rather than in an "AI revenue" line. It is the cleanest buyer-side number in the screen because Airbnb discloses the unit cost itself.

Q2 2026, verbatim: "nearly 45 percent of issues that begin with our AI assistant are now resolved without a human agent"; "customer support related cost per booking declined approximately 16 percent year-over-year ... driven in part by improvements to our AI assistant"; revenue $3.6B, +17%; nights and seats booked +10%; adjusted EBITDA $1.3B, +21%, 35% margin ([Q2 2026 results](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-q2-2026-financial-results)). Q1 2026: "over 40% of issues are now resolved without a human agent"; cost per booking down about 10% ([Q1 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001559720/000119312526211816/d23351dex991.htm)). A voice assistant for phone calls is due "later this year". Dollar support expense: no number found on the press page, so the margin impact in basis points is unquantified.

The only sell-side AI framing found runs the other way: UBS (Oct 2025, Neutral, PT $145) treats ChatGPT travel search as a disintermediation risk and calls Airbnb merely "most insulated" ([Insider Monkey](https://www.insidermonkey.com/blog/ubs-lowers-airbnb-abnb-pt-to-145-cites-chatgpt-user-adoption-concerns-1636370/)). No bank AI basket holds it. Price $172.01, about +26% YTD, trailing P/E 38.9, forward 30.4, 52-week range $110.81-193.45 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/abnb/)): a consumer multiple on a consumer story.

What kills it: the decline is "driven in part" by the assistant, so attribution is partial and the other drivers are unstated. A Q3 print where cost per booking stops falling, or the FY adjusted EBITDA margin guide (at least 35.5%) is cut while volumes hold, would say the lever is exhausted. And if agentic travel search takes bookings, a cheaper support desk does not matter.

**11. Unisys (NYSE: UIS). Avoid. The cleanest measured number for AI repricing a per-unit service, and the market already knows it.**

Unisys sells level-1 and level-2 IT help desk and PC field services per seat and per ticket. The password, access and VPN ticket mix is what AI auto-resolves first, so at every renewal Unisys hands the saving to the client. Revenue deflates, gross margin rises, and new contracts are written on "automation and service experience outcomes" instead of ticket counts. The first report inferred this for TCS, Infosys and Concentrix; Unisys is the only company where management says it in those words.

Q1 2026: Digital Workplace Solutions revenue $118M, down 6.5% in constant currency; ex-licensing gross margin up 170 basis points; CEO: "AI efficiency gains reset market pricing last year" ([Q1 transcript](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/q1-2026-earnings-transcript.pdf)). Q2 2026: DWS revenue $141.9M, down 1.1% constant currency; DWS gross margin 10.8% versus 16.9% a year earlier; roughly $1.7B of renewals repriced in 2025; CEO: "the terminology used is AI deflation... the pricing pressure is real" ([Q2 transcript](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/q2-2026-earnings-transcript.pdf)). The same quarter wrote off the full remaining DWS goodwill, $47.2M ([Q2 release](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/2q-2026-072926.pdf)).

Market cap $184.5M, price $2.53, forward P/E 2.7, no bank basket, no sell-side AI coverage ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/uis/)). The value is the read-through, not a position: a unit-priced seller loses revenue at renewal, keeps some as margin, and the goodwill gets written off. Management says the pressure is "about through" and the Q2 margin collapse was mostly transition cost on a 14,000-restaurant rollout. The test is the Q3 print on 9 Nov 2026: DWS constant-currency back above zero with gross margin recovering toward 16% would make the deflation a one-cycle reset.

**12. C.H. Robinson (NASDAQ: CHRW). Long. Narrative is consensus, price is not: adjusted operating margin 31.1% to 34.7% with headcount down 12%, stock down 12% YTD.**

A freight broker's cost base is people reading emails and PDFs and keying quotes, orders, appointments and tracking updates. LLM agents now do that extraction, so gross profit per head rises while the freight cycle masks it. Management brands this "Lean AI", so the mechanism is public; what is not public is the price agreeing with it.

Q2 2026 8-K: consolidated average headcount 11,599 versus 13,166 (-11.9%); NAST (North American Surface Transportation) headcount -11.6%; "productivity improvements of over 60% since the end of 2022 in both NAST and Global Forwarding"; NAST adjusted operating margin 40.4% (+250bp); NAST volume +1.5% against a Cass index at -3.3%, the 13th straight quarter of outgrowth ([Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000104327726000027/ex991earningsreleaseq20630.htm)). Group adjusted operating margin 34.7% versus 31.1% ([Q2 deck](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001043277/000104327726000027/q22026earningsdeck.htm)). On 5 Oct 2026 it agreed to buy RXO for $5.8B, selling the deal on about $300M of "Lean AI" synergies ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1043277/000119312526413203/d115124dex991.htm)).

Price $141.17, -12.2% YTD, 33% below the $210.33 high, down 10.85% on deal day, forward P/E 20.2, consensus Buy with average target $192 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/chrw/)). This is a mispricing of a known story (freight-cycle plus deal-risk discount), not an undiscovered mechanism. What kills it: the company bundles lean process work, severance ($26.8M in H1 2026) and AI without a split; personnel expense rose 0.9% in Q2, so the lever is heads not dollars; and Q2 revenue +19.3% was spot-rate pricing, which will flatter the per-head metric. Q3 prints 28 Oct 2026; RXO integration adds share-count and leverage risk through 1H27.

**16. US government is the largest non-law-firm employer of lawyers. Avoid the "every corporate saves on legal" thesis. No instrument.**

The generic AI thesis says corporates save on legal spend. BLS staffing data say corporates do not employ many lawyers: they buy legal work through law firms, whose revenue and rates are already in the consensus report. The in-house lawyers outside law firms sit in government, so the buyer-side margin story is a public-budget story, not a corporate one.

BLS OEWS May 2023, Lawyers: 731,340 employed; Legal Services 445,110; Local Government 57,160; State Government 47,650; a federal line of 40,630; Management of Companies 22,110, or 0.8% of the occupation ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes231011.htm)). State plus local alone is 104,810, about 14% of all lawyers and five times the corporate-HQ count; the 2024 industry split is no number found. Insurance claims litigation, the other big buyer, is already shrinking for non-AI reasons: P&C defence and cost containment fell to 3.9% of premiums in 2023-24 from about 4.5% in 2015-20 ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/10/30/845687.htm)).

The only proxy is Tyler Technologies, down about 25% YTD at $390, framed by its own sell-side as an AI-risk software name ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/tyl/)). Use this to narrow the legal-buyer edge to title insurers (edge 21) and claims litigation; do not trade it.

**18. AMN Healthcare (NYSE: AMN), Language Services. Short the segment, not the stock. Per-minute interpretation pricing down 8% with flat volume.**

AMN sells hospital interpretation by the minute (Stratus video). Hospitals buy under ACA Section 1557, which requires a "qualified interpreter" but permits machine translation with human review for routine content, so the rule caps rather than stops per-minute deflation. AMN's own purchase of Jaide Health (AI interpreting) cannibalises its per-minute book.

Q2 2026 8-K: Language Services revenue $70M, -8% year-on-year; Technology & Workforce Solutions segment $87M, -15%, gross margin 48.6% versus 55.1%; Q3 segment guide -11% to -13% ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001142750/000114275026000008/amn-ex991x20260630xearning.htm)). Call summaries decompose the language line as volume flat and pricing -8%, with pricing expected to "remain a headwind"; the primary transcript was not retrievable. The line is $70M of $673M group revenue, about 10%.

The stock is +204.6% YTD at $48.01, at its 52-week high, forward P/E 56.5, on a nurse-staffing story with zero AI framing in any 2026 headline ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMN/); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/amn/statistics/)). What kills the segment thesis: management frames the price cut as competitive tiering, never AI; and the 650bp margin drop belongs to the whole segment, which also carries VMS revenue -20%. Too small to move the group P&L either way.

**19. Robert Half (NYSE: RHI). Short. The admin and customer-support hours line is the only one still shrinking, and the stock is at 37x trailing on a cyclical recovery.**

Contract talent is billed per hour of bookkeeping, AP/AR, data entry and admin support, the occupations with 70-77% automation share in the usage data. Each automated hour is revenue removed, and the admin line should shrink faster than finance and accounting.

FY2025 10-K: service revenue $5.38B, -7.2%; contract talent -11.0% "primarily due to a 14.1% decrease in the number of hours worked"; cause given as macro only ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315213/000031521326000006/rhi-20251231.htm)). Q2 2026: finance and accounting -0.7%; administrative and customer support $154.9M, -6.5% ([Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315213/000031521326000040/rhiq22026ex991.htm)). Q3 2026: total $1,359.3M, +0.4%; F&A +2.2%; admin and customer support $154.4M, -2.7%; technology +4.6%; perm placement +6.2% ([Q3 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/315213/000031521326000049/rhiq32026ex991.htm)). Neither release mentions AI outside the risk factors.

Price $45.87, +68.9% YTD, +21.7% on the Q3 print, trailing P/E 37.6, forward 25.4, above the mean target of $39.56 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rhi/statistics/)). The AI-loser side is a published Goldman Sell ($33); what is fresh is that the market is explicitly rejecting it (Jefferies: "AI skills demand" as a tailwind). What kills it: the admin decline narrowed from -6.5% to -2.7% in one quarter, which fits a plain cyclical recovery as well as automation; the attribution is ours. Short interest is already 21.9% of float.

**20. ExlService (NASDAQ: EXLS). Long. The BPO that did not deflate: revenue +15.6% organic while the sector is tagged uninvestable.**

Same industry as TTEC, opposite result. EXL sells insurance and healthcare process outcomes and data plumbing rather than agent hours, so when AI goes into production clients move to fixed-fee and outcome contracts instead of demanding per-seat cuts.

Q2 2026 8-K: revenue $594.8M, +15.6% reported, +15.9% constant currency, no acquisition contribution; FY26 guide raised to $2.390-2.415B (+14-16%), organic 13-14% ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1297989/000129798926000021/exlq226pressrelease.htm)). The "data and AI-led revenue +28%, 60% of total" figure is from the Q1 call only and includes a planned migration from digital operations (-2%), so it is partly reclassification. Adjusted operating margin moved 19.6% to 19.7% in Q2: the "margin per outcome rises" claim is barely visible yet.

Price $42.30, flat YTD, after a 41% intra-year drawdown to $24.85 on BPO-disruption fears and a 21% rebound into the 27 Oct Q3 print; forward P/E 16.5-17.8; seven analysts, target about 5% above price ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/EXLS/); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/exls/statistics/)). The dominant 2026 frame is "AI disruption fears overshadow strong results". What kills it: Q3 organic growth below the 13% guide floor, or margin compressing as fixed-fee contracts absorb AI cost.

**21. First American Financial (NYSE: FAF). Long. Title examination is file retrieval; FAF owns the title plants and is automating 40% of refinance decisions. Not yet in the numbers.**

Title examination is locating and reviewing records against a proprietary title plant. FAF's Sequoia platform automates the title decision and Endpoint automates escrow; the labour disappears while the scarce input (county-level plant data FAF owns, plus filed premium rates) stays with FAF. Title personnel is about 28% of segment revenue, so every automated order is direct margin, if the savings arrive.

Q2 2026 call: Sequoia refinance automation 35% to 40% in the quarter; about 16% of purchase orders get instant title decisioning at opening; targets about 70% purchase and 80% refinance where FAF holds plants; Endpoint escrow automation 30% (Q1) to 34% (Q2) to 39% (July); national rollout 2027 ([transcript](https://s21.q4cdn.com/992793803/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/FAF-2026-2Q.pdf)). Q3 2026 8-K: revenue $2,084.6M, +5%; title personnel costs $574.1M, +6%; adjusted pretax title margin 12.7% versus 12.9%; closed orders 134,900 versus 141,800 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001472787/000119312526427848/faf-ex99_1.htm)). Savings per order, examiner headcount: no number found.

Price $64.72, +5.3% YTD against the S&P 500 at +11.4%, forward P/E 9.6, 3.77% yield ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/faf/statistics/)). On the Q2 call none of the four analysts asked about automation; the only AI framing the CEO cited was people "worried about title plants going away", the opposite direction. Graded company_claim because the KPIs are unaudited and personnel costs are rising. What kills it: a 2027 print where title margin is still 12-13% with automation above 50%.

**22. Aon (NYSE: AON). Long, weakest in the set. Brokers have a higher automatable task share than carriers, and the only hard headcount number is at a private peer.**

Insurance brokerage is policy servicing, certificates, renewals and correspondence. Morgan Stanley's 5 Jan 2026 model puts broker automation at 25.1% versus carriers' 21.6%, a 2030 margin uplift of 350bp versus 176bp, and an implied ~$17,000 saving per Aon sales agent on ~$82,000 ([Insurance Journal](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/01/14/854274.htm)). Acrisure, private and the 8th-largest broker, cut 2,250 roles (about 11%) on 20 May 2026, attributed to AI ([Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/claims/ai-is-cutting-insurance-jobs--the-industry-is-just-starting-to-say-so-580795.aspx)).

Aon's own numbers do not show it. Q2 2026: 5% organic growth, adjusted operating margin 28.9% versus 28.2%, credited to the Accelerating Aon United restructuring and in line with pre-existing 70-80bp guidance; no AI saving disclosed ([release](https://ir.aon.com/image/AON_Q2_2026_Earnings_Release.pdf)). Price $303.52, 52-week change -11.5%, forward P/E 15.9 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/aon/statistics/)).

Ranked last because the mechanism rests on a projection built from the same task data this screen uses, which is circular, and the Acrisure number is a press claim from a leveraged roll-up with debt reasons to cut. What makes it real: a public broker disclosing an AI-attributed headcount or expense number. None has.

### Non-US: the same mechanism where nobody has written it up

**2. Netweb Technologies (NSE: NETWEB). Long. AI Systems revenue +1,068% year-on-year, reported as a segment, and the stock is 26% below its high.**

India's IndiaAI Mission GPU compute and enterprise buildouts are procured from a domestic server integrator rather than Dell or Supermicro. Netweb is the listed pure play, and it reports AI Systems as its own segment, so Indian AI capex shows up as a line in its P&L rather than as an inference.

Q2 FY27 (quarter to 30 Sep 2026, released 24 Oct 2026): revenue INR 13,233M, +335.7% year-on-year, +61.4% sequentially; AI Systems INR 7,731M, 58.4% of revenue, +1,068.1%; HPC INR 2,482M; Private Cloud INR 2,448M; operating EBITDA margin 14.9%; PAT INR 1,367M, +334.8%; order book INR 26,290M plus L1 INR 6,525M; net cash INR 136M after a INR 12,000M QIP ([press release](https://netwebindia.com/investors/board-meeting/2026-27/Q2/Press_release.pdf)). Q1 FY27: revenue +172.1%, AI Systems +484.2% ([Q1 release](https://netwebindia.com/investors/board-meeting/2026-27/Q1/Press-Release-302026.pdf)). H1 PAT already exceeds all of FY26. Margins held at 14.7-15.1% while revenue quadrupled, so it is volume, not price. Customer concentration and IndiaAI tender share: no number found.

Price INR 4,290, +38% YTD, 52-week change only +15.9%, 26% below the Aug 2026 high of 5,813; four analysts; trailing P/E 67, forward 57, about 45x the Q2 run-rate ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/NETWEB/)). No US AI basket or ETF holds Indian small-cap hardware. What kills it: the growth is government and neocloud procurement, so a tender pause or one large customer slipping would halve the run-rate; and Indian retail already knows it as an "AI server" name, so the domestic price is not naive even if the global one is.

The narrative is fully consensus in Asia (16 analysts, 15 Buy or better, average target NT$5,803) and Alchip led the 2024-25 Taiwan ASIC rally; what is non-consensus is the price failing to move with a 6x monthly print. What kills it: this is a single-program trough-to-peak swing, not steady share gain; margin compression to the low 20s is guided; and if the customer dual-sources the next generation the September run-rate has no sequel.

### Over-punished: sold off on an AI story the filings contradict

**14. Ipsos (EPA: IPS). Long. The cheapest number in the screen: 6.5x forward, 5.6% yield, EV/EBITDA below its whole five-year range, with organic growth positive.**

### Bottleneck shift: when the cheap thing moves the constraint

**6. Figma (NYSE: FIG). Long, as a divergence trade. Accelerated to +48% while the stock fell by a third, traded as the victim of the thing feeding it.**

If generating a working front end costs cents, the binding constraint on shipping software moves upstream, to deciding what to build and what it should look like. Figma sells that layer by the seat plus AI credits. The consensus story is that seat-priced application software decelerates under AI; Figma accelerated for three straight quarters. The "design is the bottleneck" framing is the CEO's; the acceleration is a filed number.

Q2 2026 8-K: revenue $370.1M, +48%, "third straight quarter of accelerated year-over-year growth" (Q3'25 +38%, Q4'25 +40%, Q1'26 +46%); net dollar retention 136%; first full quarter of AI-credit monetisation; more than 80% of >$10k-ARR customers consuming credits weekly; FY2026 guide raised to $1.463-1.467B (about 39%); non-GAAP gross margin 85%, up 2.5 points on credits ([Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001579878/000162828026053331/q226pressrelease.htm)). AI credit revenue in dollars: no number found; the CFO declined to split the raise between credits and seats ([call highlights](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/figma-q2-earnings-call-highlights)).

Price $24.64, -34% YTD and -54% over one year; market cap $13.1B, about 9x FY2026 guided revenue; sell-side 4 Buy, 5 Hold, 0 Sell; no bank basket ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/FIG/); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fig/)). Google's Stitch launch took it down 8% in a day and Claude Design 7% ([TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/figma-fig-stock-drop-ai-competitors)). What kills it: Q3 guide is $373-375M (+36%), so the acceleration is already guided to end; Figma cut AI credit prices about 50% (Goldman Communacopia, Sept 2026), making credits a volume play; and at 9x sales and GAAP loss-making it falls with any software de-rating regardless of its numbers.

**7. Federal-court pro se filing surge. Datapoint, no instrument. Self-represented filings 11% to 16.8% since GenAI, docket burden +158%.**

LLMs make drafting a complaint free, so self-represented filings surge; the constraint moves to clerks, judges and defence counsel, who must read, docket and answer every filing. Two independent datasets measure it. Nobody has mapped it to a ticker.

Shah and Levy (MIT/USC, March 2026), 4.5M non-prisoner federal civil cases FY2005-26 plus 46M docket entries: pro se share from a long-run 11% to 16.8% in FY2025; pro se docket entries per court in the first 180 days +158% versus the pre-AI mean; AI-flagged complaints from about 0% to more than 18% in 2026 on a 1,600-complaint sample ([paper](https://avshah1.github.io/assets/pdf/papers/pro-se/Pro_Se_Automation.pdf)). Cohen-Sasson (U Miami, May 2026), 2.8M filings: pro se plaintiff rate 11.33% to 16.94%, 13.9% AI-flagged, no win-rate gain, more dismissals ([arXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.29493)). Fisher Phillips: pro se employment suits +49% in 2025, defence cost 10-15% higher; Seyfarth: pro se Fair Housing suits +69% in the first nine months of 2025 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation-finance/big-law-grapples-with-ai-fueled-pro-se-surge-rising-legal-costs)).

Neither paper is peer reviewed and attribution is by timing plus an AI-text detector. The untested proxy, Tyler Technologies, serves state courts, not the federal CM/ECF system these studies measure; its transaction revenue was +3.5% in Q2 2026 with no e-filing volume disclosed, and a separate round-2 test of Tyler on this mechanism was killed on exactly that gap ([Tyler Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/860731/000086073126000048/a991earningsrelease-6302026.htm)). The sellers that capture the burden (employment defence firms, court operators) are private. Flagged as a mechanism to instrument when a state-court e-filing number appears.

**10. Appen (ASX: APX). Long. The new scarce input for translation is human data; China run-rate +80% on generative-AI projects at 0.8x sales, five-year return -86%.**

As per-word translation goes to zero, the largest new revenue line for language vendors is proprietary AI data: human preference data, low-resource corpora, evaluation. Appen's crowd (over 1M contractors, more than 500 languages, self-reported) sells that input; its legacy annotation book deflates in the same P&L, which is why the net print is mixed.

H1 FY26 (to 30 Jun 2026, USD): group revenue $119.9M, +17%; Appen China $76.2M, +80%, underlying EBITDA $12.1M, +316%, June annualised run-rate above $175M from $135M at end-2025, "driven by generative AI related projects" and "high-margin prebuilt datasets"; Appen Global $43.7M, -27%, EBITDA loss $4.5M, "lower volumes from traditional work"; group underlying EBITDA $5.3M; FY26 guide $270-300M revenue, 5-10% EBITDA margin ([ASX release](https://announcements.asx.com.au/asxpdf/20260827/pdf/0739d4q7lyh3w2.pdf)). Nimdzi 100 2026 ranks it #18 at $230.8M and names AI data services "the greatest areas of new revenue" ([Nimdzi](https://nimdzi.com/nimdzi-100-2026/)).

Price A$1.14, roughly +40-50% YTD but 42% off the Feb-Mar high of A$1.98; market cap A$285M; 0.8x LTM sales; two analysts, both Canaccord; five-year TSR -86.5% ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/equities/appen-ltd); [Simply Wall St](https://simplywall.st/stocks/au/software/asx-apx/appen-shares/news/appen-asxapx-valuation-check-after-wider-loss-and-new-2026-r)). Known as an "AI data" name in Australian press, but too small for any bank basket. What kills it: the filing never mentions translation, so that mechanism is ours; net growth is entirely China, which Western AI funds screen out; Global is still shrinking with client concentration; and Innodata is a listed competitor, so "only listed pure play" is wrong.

### Complement: demand for the thing AI cannot do

**8. UL Solutions (NYSE: ULS). Long. Certification testing revenue +10.2% on AI racks and power supplies, capex raised for lab capacity, stock -7% YTD.**

AI designs products and data-centre hardware faster than anyone can trust them; a legally required third-party safety mark on each power supply, rack and server is a human-in-the-loop step that scales with the hardware buildout. UL is the mark. Private equity is paying £9.5B for peer Intertek; the public market is not paying for ULS.

Q2 2026 8-K: revenue $816M, +5.2%, organic +6.6%; Certification Testing revenue $237M versus $215M (+10.2%); Ongoing Certification Services $270M, +8.0%; Industrial organic +7.2%; adjusted EBITDA margin 26.8% versus 25.4%; FY2026 capex about 8.5% of revenue versus about 6.5% in 2025 ([8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1901440/000190144026000021/exhibit991q22026ulsolution.htm)). Bureau Veritas H1 organic +5.0%, guide raised ([BV](https://group.bureauveritas.com/newsroom/delivering-our-commitments-higher-sequential-organic-growth-q2)); SGS: no number verified.

Price $73.44, -6.9% YTD, near the bottom of a $63.17-107.54 range, trailing P/E 29.6, EV/EBITDA 20.6; 12 analysts, 7 Buy, average target $95.88, framed on margins not AI; Bureau Veritas trades near its high, so the unpriced claim is ULS-specific ([chartrow](https://chartrow.com/quote/uls/trailing-returns); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/uls/statistics/)). **15. Cyber and AI-liability insurers: Beazley (LSE: BEZ), Hiscox, Chubb. Avoid. The incumbents are excluding AI risk, not pricing it, and cyber rates have fallen 13 quarters running.**

The thesis said AI risk raises demand for insurance. The 2026 numbers say the largest cyber writer is shrinking its book into a soft market, and the big P&C carriers are winning approval to exclude AI liability rather than write it. The complement value, if any, is leaking to private specialists with no disclosed premium.

Beazley H1 2026: insurance written premiums $3,050.6M, -4.3%; cyber gross premiums $524.9M versus $620.3M (-15.4%); cyber rate change -4.3%; cyber undiscounted combined ratio 91.7% versus 79.7%; management: "excess competition in some markets, particularly North America"; AI risks stated as currently excluded from cyber policies ([RNS](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/beazley--bez/beazley-plc-results-for-period-ended-30-june-2026/9704869)). Hiscox is +27.8% YTD and Chubb +14.8%, both near highs at about 12.5-12.9x trailing, so the market is not trading them as AI losers either ([Yahoo HSX](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HSX.L/); [Yahoo CB](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CB/)). ### Tail supplier: an unlabelled input to the buildout

**9. Comet Holding (SIX: COTN). Long. Orders +170% on RF generators for AI-driven etch; a CHF 2.6B Swiss name 26% below its high.**

Comet sells RF (radio-frequency) generators and matchboxes for plasma etch and deposition, plus X-ray inspection. ### Demand creation: the Jevons test, negative

**17. Ambient scribes and per-visit suppliers (IDEXX, NASDAQ: IDXX, as the test). Avoid the demand-creation thesis. Scribes add 0.49 visits per clinician per week.**

The thesis was that AI scribes free clinician time, so visit volume rises and per-visit suppliers (labs, diagnostics, DSOs) win. The one large measured study says the uplift is about 2% of a 25-visit week. Per-visit suppliers are growing on price and utilisation, not visits.

JAMA (Rotenstein et al., 2026;335(16):1408-1417): 8,581 clinicians at five US academic centres, 1,809 scribe adopters, difference-in-differences: +0.49 weekly visits (95% CI 0.17-0.81), -13.4 minutes EHR time and -16.0 minutes documentation time per eight scheduled patient hours, no change in after-hours EHR time ([JAMA](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.2253)). The "$167 revenue per clinician per month" figure is secondary only. IDEXX Q2 2026: revenue $1,216.6M, +9.7%, CAG Diagnostics recurring +10.3% organic, attributed in the 10-Q to consumable and reference-lab volume per visit plus net price realisation ([release](https://ir.idexx.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/414/idexx-laboratories-announces-second-quarter-results)); the US clinical-visit decline figures (-1.3%, wellness -3.4%) are from the call, not the filing.

IDXX is $560.38, -17.2% in 2026 after +65.5% in 2025, trailing P/E 39.4, no AI framing anywhere ([companiesmarketcap](https://companiesmarketcap.com/idexx-laboratories/stock-price-history/)). Not a short: IDEXX is a pricing-power story. The value is the negative finding against the widely told "documentation gets cheap, visits go up" story: measured, small, and not showing in a per-visit supplier's volume.

## Tested and rejected

The twelve most instructive of the 114 kills across both rounds, one line each, with the lens.

- DigitalOcean (DOCN), round 1: consensus. "The AI-Native Cloud", +94% YTD; growth is a handful of large inference customers ($1M+ cohort 9% to 23% of ARR), not the long tail the thesis needed.
- Hinge Health (HNGE), both rounds: consensus and evidence. +140% YTD; the 70% to 86% gross-margin jump was $16.4M of IPO stock compensation in cost of revenue; non-GAAP moved 83% to 87%.
- Trustpilot (TRST), round 1: consensus. Reuters "emerges as AI winner", four brokers on the answer-engine story, +63% YTD.

- TTEC (TTEC), round 2: both. Already the hedge funds' named call-centre short (FT, June 2026), -57% over 52 weeks; the 8-K blames "a small number of our Engage clients", not AI.
- Bandwidth (BAND), round 2: both. +186% YTD on "AI voice"; about 60% of the $40M revenue increase was pass-through messaging surcharges, core cloud comms +12%.
- Waystar (WAY), round 2: evidence, twice. The claims-arms-race thesis needs volume-based revenue to rise; it grew 3%, and provider-solutions volume revenue fell $2.1M while subscription (Iodine acquisition) grew 34%.
- Cboe (CBOE), round 2: evidence. Record SPX 0DTE volumes attributed by Cboe to uncertainty, retail and the pattern-day-trader rule repeal; no filing or call mentions AI or agents.
- Mitek (MITK), round 2: both. +77% YTD; the Fraud & Identity surge was "tied to a specific regulatory driver involving age assurance" and the total beat was renewal timing, per the CFO.
- Duolingo (DUOL), round 2: both. "Over-punished AI casualty" is the dominant press frame since June, +63% off the low; bookings decelerated 14% to 8% and paid subs 21% to 17%, which is what the bear case predicts.
- Nittobo (3110), round 2: consensus. The T-glass bottleneck is real and sold out, but the stock tripled in five months (JPY 2,040 to 6,390) with Goldman and Morgan Stanley on it; the -56% since is a crowded unwind.

The pattern across 114 kills is the same in both rounds. In roughly two-thirds the numbers were real and the AI attribution was not: the filing gave a non-AI cause (a lost customer, a regulatory driver, acquisition mix, copper pass-through, renewal timing, a platform fee, restructuring, FX) for every number the thesis needed, and management's AI language was cost-side marketing or boilerplate risk. The round-2 twist was a cluster of "margin should expand" theses (Charter, Sysco, HP, FactSet, Aon) where the company announced an AI cost programme and the relevant expense line then rose. The consensus refuter killed the remaining third, usually on the 60%-YTD rule, and often found the stock had already tripled and halved on the exact mechanism before we looked.

## How this sits next to the consensus sleeves

The first report's sleeves are toll booths priced by volume, buyers of cheapened output, rented hypergrowth and a capex hedge. These 22 add a different factor exposure. The longs are idiosyncratic: a travel marketplace's support line, an Indian server integrator, a magazine publisher's licensing mix, a Taiwanese ASIC house, a for-profit university, a design tool, a safety-mark issuer, a Swiss RF supplier. Their returns depend on a single company's next print diverging from a sector story, which is close to uncorrelated with the compute and power sleeves and with each other. The avoids and datapoints (Unisys, cyber insurers, government lawyers, scribes) sharpen the first report's "avoid seat, hour and click pricing" sleeve by showing what deflation and non-demand look like in a filing.

What they do not add is size or liquidity. Market caps run from A$285M (Appen) and $184M (Unisys) through $1.5-16B for most, with two of the clearest numbers (Netweb, Alchip) on exchanges without ADRs and three (pro se, government lawyers, scribes) with no instrument at all. Coverage is two to five analysts in several cases. All of it was found by web search over earnings releases, transcripts and price pages, not by reading every filing in the universe, and the round-2 search budget ran out before bank-basket membership could be checked by search rather than by fetch. The 22-of-136 hit rate says more about how strict the attribution test was than about how many edges exist, and the next prints (EXLS 27 Oct, CHRW 28 Oct, TYL 29 Oct, ULS 3 Nov, LOPE 4 Nov, HRB 5 Nov, UIS 9 Nov) will retire several of these within a month. This is research on public filings, not personalised advice.
