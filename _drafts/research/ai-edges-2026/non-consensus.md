# Non-consensus addendum: two survivors from forty tested

*Addendum to "AI Value Flows to Volume, Not Seats". Research, not personalised advice. Prices as of 23 Oct 2026 unless stated.*

## Method

The first report was judged "way too consensus", so this pass started from the opposite end. Bottom-up, we took measured AI usage (which tasks are actually being automated or augmented, by occupation, from the Anthropic Economic Index and the OpenAI usage paper) and asked which listed company sells that task by the unit, or buys it. Top-down, we ran six contrarian classes against the same data: bottleneck shift (the cheapened input moves the constraint somewhere else), over-punished (sold off on an AI story the filings contradict), tail supplier (an unlabelled input to the buildout), complement (demand for the thing AI cannot do), demand creation (Jevons: cheaper output, more of it), and non-US (the same mechanism on a market where nobody has written it up).

That produced roughly 126 candidates. 86 were dropped for budget before verification. 40 went through two independent refuters: a consensus refuter (is it in a Goldman, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, UBS or Citi AI basket; how many 2026 sell-side or press pieces frame it as an AI play; is it up 60%+ year-to-date on that story) and an evidence refuter (do the numbers exist at the cited primary source, and does the source attribute them to AI or to something else). The default rule was strict: loose attribution means refuted. Two of 40 survived. Both are below, ranked mechanically by evidence quality times non-consensus times clarity of mechanism, which puts the measured datapoint above the tradeable long.

## The ranked edges

### Unit-priced seller, deflation side: the one place "AI deflation" is in a filing

**1. Unisys (NYSE: UIS). Avoid. The cleanest measured number for AI repricing a per-unit service, and the market already knows it.**

Unisys sells level-1 and level-2 IT help desk and PC field services per seat and per ticket. The password, access and VPN ticket mix is what AI auto-resolves first, so at every renewal Unisys has to hand the saving to the client. Revenue deflates, gross margin rises, and new contracts are written on "automation and service experience outcomes, rather than ticket volumes" instead of ticket counts. That is the mechanism the first report inferred for TCS, Infosys and Concentrix; Unisys is the only company where management says it in those words.

The numbers are verbatim from the transcripts. Q1 2026: Digital Workplace Solutions revenue $118M, down 6.5% in constant currency; ex-licensing gross margin up 170 basis points; CEO: "AI efficiency gains reset market pricing last year"; about 600 basis points of margin gain over three years attributed to embedded AI; target of ~40% of the legacy base on the Agentic Service Desk by end-2026 ([Q1 transcript](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/q1-2026-earnings-transcript.pdf)). Q2 2026: DWS revenue $141.9M, down 1.1% constant currency; DWS gross margin 10.8% versus 16.9% a year earlier; roughly $1.7B of renewals repriced in 2025; CEO: "the terminology used is AI deflation... the pricing pressure is real" ([Q2 transcript](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/q2-2026-earnings-transcript.pdf)). The same quarter carried a $47.2M goodwill impairment, the full remaining DWS goodwill ([Q2 release](https://www.unisys.com/siteassets/investor-relations/earnings-files/2026/2q-2026-072926.pdf)).

Why this is non-consensus: it is not in any bank AI basket we could find, has no sell-side AI narrative, and the only 2026 "AI" coverage is auto-generated earnings recaps. Market cap is $184.5M, price $2.53 against a 52-week range of $1.97-4.98, forward P/E 2.7 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/uis/)), and the stock is down about 20% year-to-date ([AAII, 12 Oct](https://www.aaii.com/investingideas/article/547101-why-unisys-corporation8217s-uis-stock-is-down-539)). A $184M company on 2.7x earnings is the opposite of a crowded trade, which is exactly why the deflation reads as real rather than as a story.

What the number is worth, and what kills it. As a position this is "avoid", not a short: the price already discounts the deflation, there is a large pension overhang, and peer DXC (revenue -5.1% YoY, forward P/E 6.0) doubled from $7.90 to $16.09 in a year as the market re-rated cheap IT services on AI margin ([DXC](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxc/)). Management also says the pressure is "about through" (three-quarters of renewal cycles done by end-2026) and that ~85% of repriced deals had scope added back. The margin collapse in Q2 was mostly transition cost on a 14,000-restaurant rollout, not AI. So the value here is the read-through: a unit-priced seller loses revenue to AI at renewal, keeps some of it as margin, and the goodwill gets written off. The next test is the Q3 print on 9 Nov 2026; a DWS constant-currency number back above zero with gross margin recovering toward 16% would say the deflation is a one-cycle reset, not a trend.

### Bottleneck shift: when code is cheap, the spec is the constraint

**2. Figma (NYSE: FIG). Long, as a divergence trade. Accelerated to +48% while the stock fell a third, because it is traded as the victim of the thing that is feeding it.**

The plain-words mechanism: if generating a working front end costs cents, the binding constraint on shipping software moves upstream, to deciding what to build and what it should look like. Figma sells that layer by the seat plus AI credits. The first report's consensus story is that seat-priced application software decelerates under AI; Figma is a seat-priced vendor that accelerated for three straight quarters. That is the only non-company evidence for the "design is the bottleneck" claim, and we flag it as the CEO's framing, but the acceleration itself is a filed number.

Q2 2026 (8-K, 5 Aug 2026): revenue $370.1M, +48% year-on-year, "third straight quarter of accelerated year-over-year growth" (Q3'25 +38%, Q4'25 +40%, Q1'26 +46%); net dollar retention 136% (down from 139% in Q1); first full quarter of AI-credit monetisation; more than 80% of >$10k-ARR customers consuming AI credits weekly; FY2026 guide raised a further $40M to $1.463-1.467B (~39% growth), with the CFO calling the raise "reflective of strength in AI credit consumption for products being monetized today"; non-GAAP gross margin 85%, up 2.5 points quarter-on-quarter, attributed to credits ([Q2 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001579878/000162828026053331/q226pressrelease.htm); [Q1 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001579878/000162828026035087/q126pressrelease.htm)). Free cash flow was $53.2M (14% margin); GAAP operating margin was -32% on Config conference spend, non-GAAP +10%. AI credit revenue in dollars: no number found; Figma does not split it, and the CFO declined to split the raise between credits and seats when JPMorgan asked ([call highlights](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/figma-q2-earnings-call-highlights)).

Why the market is not pricing it: the 2026 press framing is dominantly AI-victim. Google's Stitch "vibe design" launch in March took the stock down 8% in a day; Anthropic's Claude Design launch in June took it down 7% and the stock lost more than 20% that month ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/figma-fig-stock-trending-googles-233127606.html); [TIKR](https://www.tikr.com/blog/figma-fig-stock-drop-ai-competitors)). Price was $24.64 on 23 Oct, -34% year-to-date and -54% over one year against an S&P 500 up 11% ([Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/FIG/)); two weeks earlier it was ~$22 and -42%. Market cap $13.1B, about 9x FY2026 guided revenue, 52-week low $16.60, post-IPO high $142.92 ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fig/)). No inclusion in any bank AI basket was found; the sell-side tally is 4 Buy, 5 Hold, 0 Sell, with BofA and Citi the only houses making the "AI strengthens competitive position" case. Activist Findell Capital called it undervalued in May and pushed management on the Anthropic relationship.

What kills it. First, the Q3 print: guidance is $373-375M (+36% at the midpoint), so the acceleration is already guided to end; if NDR drops below 130% while seat counts stall, the AI-credits-plus-seats story becomes an AI-credits-instead-of-seats story. Second, Figma cut AI credit prices roughly 50% (Goldman Communacopia, Sept 2026), which means credits are a volume play not a pricing tailwind, so revenue per customer has to come from consumption growth. Third, this is 9x sales and GAAP loss-making, so it is a divergence trade (fundamentals up, price down), not a value trade; a second software de-rating would take it down with the group regardless of its numbers. Fourth, the mechanism is not isolated: how much of the 48% is AI versus seat expansion on a 2025-IPO-era base is undisclosed, so the "value moved to the design layer" claim rests on management framing plus one acceleration.

## Tested and rejected

The ten most instructive of the 38 kills, one line each, with the refuter that killed them.

- DigitalOcean (DOCN): consensus. Branded "the AI-Native Cloud", +94% YTD, and the growth is a handful of large inference customers ($1M+ cohort 9% to 23% of ARR; RPO $894M with 3.7-year duration), not the long tail of cheap projects the thesis needed.
- Hinge Health (HNGE): consensus and evidence. +128% over 52 weeks with five target raises in two weeks; the headline gross margin jump from 70% to 86% was $16.4M of IPO stock compensation in cost of revenue, non-GAAP moved 83% to 87%.
- Trustpilot (TRST): consensus. Reuters headline "emerges as AI winner", UBS, RBC, Peel Hunt, JPMorgan all on the AEO (answer-engine optimisation) story, +63% YTD; the mechanism is real but the whole covering universe owns it.
- Wiley (WLY): consensus. AI-licensing is the dominant press frame, roughly +70% YTD; the 130bp Research margin gain is attributed by the CFO to the Emerald acquisition and cost programmes, not AI copyediting.
- ZoomInfo (GTM): consensus. Jefferies, Stifel, BTIG, Canaccord, Morgan Stanley and Piper all published the seat-compression short; -57% YTD, 16% short float, consensus Hold with target 5% above price.
- Future plc (FUTR): consensus. Jefferies, Barclays and Stifel all downgraded on AI search in 2026, Press Gazette and The Drum ran the zero-click story; the affiliate line is only ~16% of revenue, so the short is on a slice plus multiple compression.
- VeriSign (VRSN): consensus and evidence. Citi modelled "AI-related demand as a key driver" in February; the 10-Q lists AI as one of three co-drivers next to a +30% rise in Verisign's own registrar marketing spend, with first-time renewal rates in the mid-40s.
- Quest Diagnostics (DGX): evidence. The 14.5% requisition growth looked like the chatbot-to-blood-panel Jevons signature, but the CFO attributed 9.5 points to the Corewell and Fresenius lab-management deals; ex those, volume grew 5%, and the stock is +42% YTD anyway.
- Intertek (ITRK): evidence and instrument. Business Assurance grew 10% but the filing credits supply-chain and sustainability audits, ISO 42001 is a single bullet, and shareholders approved EQT's 6,107.7p cash takeover in August, so a long is now a merger-arb spread.
- Forrester (FORR): evidence. The "usage up, revenue down" cannibalisation story has the wrong sign: contract value went from -7% to -3% as AI usage doubled, retention rose, and AI Access is a separate ~$10M product sold on top.

The pattern across the 38: in about two-thirds the numbers were real and the AI attribution was not. Filings gave a non-AI cause (acquisition mix, cycle, outreach deals, a divestiture, a takeover) for every number the thesis needed, and management AI language was cost-side or boilerplate. The consensus refuter killed the rest, usually because the stock was up 60%+ YTD on the same story.

## Thin

Neither survivor is thin in the sense the structure anticipated; both are graded "measured" and their core numbers were verified in primary filings. The thin part is narrower: Figma's "design is the bottleneck" is management narrative supported by one acceleration, and Unisys's "pressure is about through" is a CEO forecast. Treat the mechanism claims as watch items and the filed numbers as the evidence.

The more useful watchlist is the set that passed the consensus refuter and failed only on attribution, since a single disclosure could flip each. Omada Health (OMDA): gross margin 49.4% to 72.6% in two years while the stock is -24% over one year, 14.6% short float, no AI attribution in any release. Teladoc BetterHelp (TDOC): cash-pay revenue -12% and insurance demand that "outpaced available provider capacity", exactly the chatbot-takes-low-acuity pattern, but the 8-K never says why cash-pay fell and marketing was cut 17%. UHS via Talkspace: the escalation thesis has no number because the deal closed 17 Aug 2026 and Q3 is the first post-close print (due 27 Oct). Genpact (G): Core Business Services +1.9% versus Advanced Technology +24%, 73% of revenue in the unit-priced bucket, no numeric core guide; the segment-migration confound is unresolved. Informa (INF): B2B live events +8% underlying with the stock -6% YTD, but the RNS attributes nothing to inbox saturation. Certara (CERT): sold its regulatory-writing unit for $100M firm (~5.9x EBITDA) with no stated AI reason. NerdWallet (NRDS): credit-card revenue down $8.6M on "organic search traffic", the word AI absent, Q3 due 5 Nov. None of these is a position; each is one disclosure away from being one.

## How this sits next to the consensus sleeves

The first report's sleeves are toll booths priced by volume, buyers of cheapened output, rented hypergrowth and a capex hedge. These two names add a different factor: Figma is an idiosyncratic long whose return depends on a single company's Q3 print diverging from a sector de-rating, which is close to uncorrelated with the compute and power sleeves; Unisys is a datapoint that sharpens the "avoid seat, hour and click pricing" sleeve by showing what the deflation looks like in a filing, down to the goodwill write-off. What they do not add is liquidity or coverage: one is a $13B 2025 IPO with lockup supply and no basket sponsorship, the other a $184M micro-cap with no sell-side AI coverage at all, and a two-name survivor set is too small to call a factor. The caveat on all of it: these were found by web search over earnings releases, transcripts and price pages, not by reading every filing in the universe, and 86 candidates were dropped unverified for budget; the two-of-forty hit rate says more about how strict the attribution test was than about how few edges exist.
